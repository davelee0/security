# Child Safeguarding x Data Security: Under-served Intersections for Small Child-Serving Orgs

Research method note (2026-11-23): Direct page fetching was blocked in this session (missingkids.org and archspm.org failed to resolve), so findings below rely on web-search result snippets and summaries. Each claim is cited to the URL the search returned; where a snippet was truncated or a date was unclear, that is flagged. Readers should spot-check primary sources before publishing figures.

## Targeted-individual threats: non-custodial parents, abusers, protective-order subjects locating a child via school/childcare records, rosters, pickup systems, apps, photos, or front-office social engineering

### Takeaway
This is the most concrete, under-served safeguarding-security overlap for small orgs: the legal tools exist (court orders, FERPA directory opt-outs, state Address Confidentiality Programs, McKinney-Vento/foster confidentiality), but they only work if the org's records, vendors, apps, photo practices and front-office procedures actually honor a "do not disclose" flag across every system. Small parishes, schools and daycares rarely have a process for that, and pickup/roster/bus apps have repeatedly leaked exactly the "where is this child at 3pm" data an abuser wants.

### Cited Findings
**Legal baseline / records access**
- FERPA and most state laws give both parents equal access to a child's school records by default; an abusive non-custodial parent may request records to find the address or workplace of the abused parent or child. Access can be cut off only when a restraining order says so or a court denies parenting time — [MassLegalHelp](https://masslegalhelp.org/es/node/1317); see also [WomensLaw NY](https://www.womenslaw.org/laws/ny/custody/after-custody-order-place/can-parent-who-does-not-have-custody-have-access-childs) (NY access depends on the custody order).
- Safety guidance: identifying/locating information (school name, addresses, workplace, phone numbers) should generally not be released to abusers, and records should be redacted of anything that could locate a parent/child victim — [MassLegalHelp](https://masslegalhelp.org/es/node/1317).
- A school can only act on an order it knows about; the parent must deliver a copy. Virginia's model policy has the principal document the order's receipt date, expiration date and who provided it in the student record — [Virginia DOE Attachment A](https://doe.virginia.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/37255/638060299668570000).
- Missouri: if the custodial parent is in the state Address Confidentiality Program, the judge must order that records given to the non-custodial parent omit the parent's and child's address — [WomensLaw MO](https://www.womenslaw.org/laws/mo/custody/after-custody-order-issued/can-non-custodial-parent-have-access-childs-records).
- Advocates for Children of New York publishes guidance on supporting students living in DV shelters (2024) — [AFC NY PDF](https://advocatesforchildren.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/supporting_students_in_dv_shelters.pdf) (contents not fetched).

**FERPA directory information opt-out**
- FERPA requires public notice of directory-information categories and "a reasonable period" to opt out; PTAC publishes a model notice — [studentprivacy.ed.gov model notice](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/resources/model-notice-directory-information).
- NNEDV's Safety Net project warns the opt-out window can be "just a week or two when school first starts," and many schools require re-opting out each year — [NNEDV TechSafety](https://techsafety.org/blog/2015/8/20/increasing-privacy-opt-out-of-schools-sharing-kids-information) (2015, still cited).
- World Privacy Forum's 2020 study: only 39% of studied K-12 schools post FERPA opt-out forms online; WPF argues opt-outs must be allowed on an ongoing basis and lists DV/stalking victims as a group for whom opting out is essential — [WPF Without Consent](https://worldprivacyforum.org/?p=7233); [WPF summary PDF](https://www.worldprivacyforum.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/ferpa/without_consent_2020_summary.pdf). Consumer Reports covered the same "directory/yearbook loophole" (undated in results) — [Consumer Reports](https://www.consumerreports.org/electronics/privacy/student-information-exposed-by-legal-loophole-a5216747520/).
- CRS published a FERPA exceptions primer dated 2026-01-15 — [CRS IF13155](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/IF13155.html).
- I found no PTAC document specifically addressing DV survivors or an automatic opt-out for them (gap).

**New York Address Confidentiality Program (ACP)**
- NY Executive Law §108 ACP covers DV victims and household members including minor children; "actual address" explicitly includes a **school address** — [NY DOS statute](https://dos.ny.gov/nys-acp-statute); [About program](https://dos.ny.gov/about-program); [FAQ Aug 2025](https://dos.ny.gov/address-confidentiality-program-frequently-asked-questions-august-2025).
- NYSED Commissioner decision (Appeal of Jane Doe, No. 18,456, ~2024) notes a school district is not entitled to a participant's actual address without Secretary of State permission (side observation, not the holding) — [NYSED decision](https://counsel.nysed.gov/Decisions/volume64/d18456).
- No source found on how a school/daycare should record a child enrolled under an ACP substitute address, or how that propagates to vendors (SIS, bus, pickup, photo, yearbook).

**McKinney-Vento and foster youth**
- NCHE brief: FERPA protections help ensure students/families cannot be tracked by an abuser through school records — [NCHE DV & Homelessness brief](https://resources.rhyttac.org/sites/default/files/document/NCHE_Domestic%20Violence%20Homelessness%20Education%20Services.pdf).
- NAEHCY: a McKinney-Vento student's address and living-situation information cannot be released without consent, even where directory info could be — [NAEHCY Privacy & Homelessness](https://naehcy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Privacy-and-Homelessness-FERPA-2016.pdf).
- Foster care status must be kept confidential and SIS flags access-limited (WA OSPI 2023) — [OSPI Q&A](https://ospi.k12.wa.us/sites/default/files/2023-11/foster-care-liaison-q-and.pdf); Texas: keep records secure from anyone who doesn't need to know — [TEA FCSS guide ch.6](https://tea.texas.gov/sites/default/files/2022-fcss-resource-guide-chapter-06.pdf).
- Practice guidance: ensure all staff who might be contacted by an alleged abuser understand confidentiality (i.e., front-office social engineering is a recognized vector) — [TEA/search summary](https://tea.texas.gov/system/files/fcss_rg-ch06.pdf).

**Documented misuse / location-revealing systems**
- Chicago Tribune/Columbian (July 2024): a father who moved out of state ran Facebook posts offering money for information on his children and bought **targeted Facebook ads geofenced to a ½-mile radius around the children's school** seeking photos; no charges were filed despite repeated reports — [The Columbian](https://columbian.com/news/2024/jul/14/i-want-her-to-worry-about-whos-waiting-on-the-corner-how-one-man-uses-facebook-to-frighten-his-childrens-mother-and-why-police-do-nothing).
- MIT Technology Review (June 2025): ex-partner kept location tracking on a daughter's smartwatch; family court didn't treat it as decisive — [MIT TR](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/06/18/1118235/big-tech-intimate-partner-violence/amp/).
- HopHop (Quebec school/daycare pickup app): researcher found exposure of children's names, guardians' names, children's photos and school; also possible fraudulent "parent arriving" notifications; the province banned it (date unclear in results, ~2023) — [Daily Security Review](https://dailysecurityreview.com/cyber-security/quebec-hophop-app-leak-exposes-childrens-data-across-hundreds-of-schools-and-daycares/); [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/montreal/article/quebec-school-app-pulled-offline-after-major-security-flaw-exposes-parents-data/).
- Tenable (2023): Edulog bus-tracking portal flaws exposed K-12 student location data; fixed by 2023-11-30 — [Tenable](https://www.tenable.com/blog/the-kids-arent-alright-vulnerabilities-in-edulog-portal-revealed-k-12-student-location-data); The 74 on GPS bus tracker risks — [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/relied-on-by-parents-hailed-by-schools-gps-bus-trackers-raise-security-risks/).
- Feb 2026: a student admissions website bug exposed children's names, DOBs, addresses, pictures and school details — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/19/bug-in-student-admissions-website-exposed-childrens-personal-information).
- Researcher found data leak in a school safety app used by Missouri districts (Jan 2024) — [KBIA](https://www.kbia.org/missouri-news-network/2024-01-12/researcher-finds-data-leak-in-safety-app-used-by-some-missouri-school-districts); potential breach of an anonymous tip app (Mar 2026) — [EdWeek](https://www.edweek.org/technology/a-potential-breach-of-an-anonymous-tip-app-could-have-exposed-sensitive-student-data/2026/03).
- Cornell Tech's Clinic to End Tech Abuse (CETA, NYC) helps survivors disable unwanted location sharing; lists FindMy, Life360, AirTags/Tile among common abuse methods and publishes a clinic toolkit — [IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/privacy-professionals-need-to-be-aware-of-tech-abuse); [USENIX Enigma 2023 slides](https://www.usenix.org/sites/default/files/conference/protected-files/enigma2023_slides_ramjit.pdf). Australia's eSafety Commissioner report on children and technology-facilitated abuse in DFV (2020) covers monitoring/stalking continuing after separation — [eSafety summary](https://www.esafety.gov.au/sites/default/files/2020-12/Children%20and%20technology-facilitated%20abuse%20-%20Summary%20report.pdf).

### Inferences
- The practical failure mode is **propagation**: a custody order or ACP status recorded on paper in the front office does not automatically suppress the child in the SIS, Brightwheel-type app, pickup app, bus app, yearbook, class photo galleries, parish directory, Flocknote list, or a teacher's Instagram. A volunteer "protected-child checklist" (every system that holds the child's name/photo/location, and how to suppress it) is an obvious, low-cost, non-legal deliverable.
- Small orgs outside public-school FERPA (many private preschools, parish religious-ed, youth sports) may not have any directory-opt-out mechanism at all; the WPF/NNEDV concerns apply even more to them.
- Front-office verification scripts (caller/visitor asking "is X in Ms. Y's class?", "what time is pickup?") are a defensive-training gap that fits a security volunteer's skills (social-engineering awareness) without needing legal expertise.
- CETA is in NYC, aligning with the project's NYC focus; a referral/partnership path for families is plausible.

### Gaps
- No documented US case found (in search snippets) of an abuser locating a child specifically through a school/parish website photo or roster leak; the Tribune case shows intent and method (geofenced ads near the school) but not a successful records leak. Likely under-reported rather than nonexistent.
- No PTAC guidance specific to DV survivors located; no NY guidance on ACP + school vendor systems.
- CETA's child/custody-specific publications were not surfaced.

## Catholic Safe Environment programs (Dallas Charter, VIRTUS, CMG Connect, Praesidium, StoneBridge audits) and digital records

### Takeaway
Safe Environment programs generate a large, sensitive dataset (volunteer SSNs/DOBs for FCRA background checks, training records, and abuse-allegation files) spread across diocesan offices, parishes, and vendors, yet publicly available audit material focuses on compliance counts, not data protection. I found no public breach of a VIRTUS/CMG/Praesidium database, but also no published vendor security/retention terms, which is itself a gap.

### Cited Findings
- USCCB 2025 CYP Annual Report (released May 2026, covers 2024-07-01 to 2025-06-30; audit by StoneBridge Business Partners): 2,328,545 adults and 2,803,250 children/youth trained; 1,070 allegations from 973 victims across 194 dioceses/eparchies — [USCCB news 2026](https://www.usccb.org/news/2026/us-bishops-secretariat-child-and-youth-protection-releases-annual-report); [Report PDF](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2025%20CYP%20Annual%20Report%20Digital%20Version.pdf).
- The 2025 report notes the Charter does not mandate renewal of background checks and safe-environment training, so renewal rules vary by diocese — [USCCB 2025 report PDF](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2025%20CYP%20Annual%20Report%20Digital%20Version.pdf) (via search summary).
- Renewal cycles vary by diocese/vendor (e.g., Selection.com/CMG Connect 5 yrs; Portland OR Praesidium 3 yrs) — [USCCB 2021 Background Check Methodologies](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2021%20Background%20Check%20Methodologies.pdf). Columbus moved to 5-year renewals for child-facing volunteers in Aug 2025 — [Diocese of Columbus](https://columbuscatholic.org/seo).
- Diocese of Arlington documentation: background-check data includes Social Security numbers, processed under FCRA; it maintains a "PII security" support article — [Arlington OPCYP PII security](https://opcypsupport.arlingtondiocese.org/support/solutions/articles/44002152724-opcyp-pii-security).
- CMG Connect is where parish site administrators review background checks and assign certification statuses (i.e., parish-level volunteers have access to background-check results) — [Diocese of St. Cloud CMG Site Admin Guide 2024](https://stcdio.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/CMG-Site-Admin-User-Guide-2024.pdf). Dioceses publish guides for parish staff to record background checks in VIRTUS — [Archdiocese of Denver 2023](https://archden.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/11/Recording-SE-Items-in-VIRTUS-2023.11.28.pdf); [Diocese of Lansing 2026](https://www.dioceseoflansing.org/sites/default/files/2026-05/Grid-Virtus-CBC-Process-2026.pdf).
- A comparable vendor (MinistrySafe/Abuse Prevention Systems) publishes a data-retention policy: 3-year retention of background-check data, encrypted PII at rest — [MinistrySafe 2025](https://ministrysafe.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/DATA-RETENTION-POLICY.pdf). No equivalent public policy found for VIRTUS, CMG Connect or Praesidium.
- The Archdiocese of St. Paul & Minneapolis posted a "Data Breach Notice" referring people to vendor Image-I-Nation (a background-screening firm); dates, scope and whether safe-environment data was involved could not be confirmed because the page could not be fetched — [ArchSPM notice](https://www.archspm.org/?p=3941); same archdiocese announced background-check package changes — [ArchSPM Mission Support](https://missionsupport.archspm.org/news/background-check-package-changes/).

### Inferences
- Attack surface is at the **parish** level: many part-time coordinators with vendor-portal logins that expose volunteer PII and clearance status; weak passwords/no MFA there would be a realistic route to identity theft of thousands of volunteers. A volunteer project could offer "safe-environment coordinator account hygiene" (MFA, offboarding when coordinators change, no spreadsheets of SSNs emailed around, secure storage of paper application forms).
- Abuse-allegation and victim-assistance records are among the most sensitive data a parish/diocese holds; leaks would re-traumatize victims and deter reporting. No public digital-handling guidance found.
- The Image-I-Nation lead is worth a follow-up (it may be a vendor breach affecting diocesan background checks).

### Gaps
- No StoneBridge audit criteria on data security found; auditor recommendations on technology in the 2025 report were not surfaced.
- No public VIRTUS/CMG/Praesidium security, retention, or breach history found.
- Details of the ArchSPM/Image-I-Nation breach unconfirmed.

## Youth ministry / youth program communication tech and adult-minor digital contact policies

### Takeaway
Dioceses have converged on "two-adult / no private 1:1 messaging / official accounts with multiple admins / parental visibility" rules, but policies are platform-agnostic, rarely mention Discord or ephemeral apps, and say little about the security of the accounts themselves (shared passwords, ex-volunteer access, MFA), which is where a security volunteer adds value.

### Cited Findings
- Diocese of San Diego policy (dated 2025-12-03): at least two adults in an official capacity must have full access to organizational accounts — [San Diego policy PDF](https://sdcatholic.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Diocesan-32-Electric-comm-minors-12.3.2025.pdf).
- Diocese of Green Bay (2025): "TWO PLUS ONE" — two background-checked, safe-environment-trained adults in any digital communication with an unrelated minor; parents may request to review communications — [Green Bay policy](https://www.gbdioc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Office-of-Safe-Environment-Digital-Communication-Policy-English-1.pdf).
- Diocese of Salina: at least two adult admins per ministry account; no personal accounts for ministry; group not individual communication; no online "chatting" with minors — [Salina guidelines](https://salinadiocese.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/social-media-for-website.pdf); [Salina 2022](https://salinadiocese.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/social-media-2.pdf).
- Episcopal Diocese of Chicago (2022): at least two unrelated adult admins plus two youth admins for youth groups — [Episcopal Chicago](https://episcopalchicago.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/09/Social-Media-Guidelines-for-Children-and-Youth.pdf).
- Insurer Brotherhood Mutual offers sample teen-texting and digital-communication policies (group messages with at least two adults) — [Brotherhood Mutual sample policy](https://www.brotherhoodmutual.com/resources/safety-library/risk-management-articles/children-and-youth/youth-ministry/youth-ministry-sample-policy-digital-communication/); NFCYM social media guidelines — [NFCYM via Saginaw](https://saginaw.org/sites/default/files/NFCYM-Social-Media-Guidelines.pdf).
- None of the policies surfaced named Discord specifically (search summary).

### Inferences
- "Two adults with admin access" can become a security weakness if implemented as one shared password; recommend role-based admin, MFA, and offboarding checklist when catechists/coordinators leave.
- Group-texting platforms (Flocknote, Remind, GroupMe) hold minors' phone numbers; the policy question ("is the conversation transparent?") is addressed, the data question ("who can export the roster?") is not.

### Gaps
- No documented security breach found of Flocknote, Remind, or similar parish/youth group-text platforms in this search (not searched exhaustively).
- No Discord-specific diocesan guidance found.

## Sextortion trends targeting minors (FBI/NCMEC 2023-2026) and use of leaked school data

### Takeaway
Financial sextortion of minors is rising steeply (NCMEC: ~50,000 financial-sextortion reports in 2025, up from ~36,000 in 2024; 40,000+ by Aug 2026 per AP), and image-scraping from school websites/social media is now documented as a sextortion input (UK, Australia, 2026). Evidence that stolen school *records* (rosters, emails) are used to pick sextortion victims is not established; documented school-data extortion targets parents/districts with record dumps rather than sexual images.

### Cited Findings
- NCMEC 2025 data: >50,000 financially motivated sextortion reports (≈137/day), up from >36,000 in 2024; at least three dozen US teen boys have died by suicide after victimization — [NCMEC blog 2026](https://us.missingkids.org/blog/2026/ncmec-releases-new-sextortion-data-2025) (via search summary; page not fetchable).
- NCMEC CyberTipline 2025: 1.4 million online-enticement reports, >80,000 concerning sextortion, >800 where offender traveled to meet the child — [NCMEC CyberTipline data](https://www.missingkids.org/cybertiplinedata) (via search summary). Note: the financial-sextortion count is a subset of enticement; do not add figures.
- AP/FRONTLINE investigation (Nov 2026): by August, NCMEC had >40,000 financial sextortion reports — [La Nación (AP takeaways)](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/takeaways-from-the-ap-and-frontline-investigation-into-sextortion-nid17112026/).
- Senate Judiciary testimony (2025-12-09, NCMEC's Coffren) — [Testimony PDF](https://www.judiciary.senate.gov/imo/media/doc/57fece5a-a8d8-35e9-aac0-294cf4561020/2025-12-09_Testimony_Coffren.pdf) (contents not fetched).
- Tennessee Bureau of Investigation (June 2026) warns of growing financial sextortion of children — [TBI](https://tbinewsroom.com/2026/06/29/tbi-warns-of-growing-financial-sextortion-threat-targeting-children/).
- Older FBI data: >13,000 reports of financial sextortion of minors Oct 2021–Mar 2023, ≥12,600 victims (mostly boys), ≥20 suicides — [search summary of FBI/DOJ coverage](https://news.yahoo.com/fbi-doj-warn-staggering-increase-170154733.html).
- Malwarebytes (May 2026): deepfake sextortion forcing UK schools to remove student photos from websites; per IWF, criminals scraped pupil photos from school sites/social media, created explicit fakes and demanded payment; Childline Report Remove took 394 blackmail reports from under-18s, up one-third on 2024 — [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/family-and-parenting/2026/05/deepfake-sextortion-forces-schools-to-remove-student-photos-from-websites).
- School-data extortion: PowerSchool (Dec 2024 breach; May 2025 extortion of individual districts, e.g., ≥20 NC districts) despite PowerSchool having paid for deletion — [EdNC](https://www.ednc.org/nc-schools-targeted-for-extortion-again-months-after-powerschool-data-breach/); [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/powerschool-paid-off-hackers-after-huge-breach-now-theyre-extorting-districts/); [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/tech/security/school-districts-hit-extortion-attempts-powerschool-breach-rcna205429). Clark County, NV (2023): hackers emailed parents their children's stolen data — [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-email-stolen-student-data-to-parents-of-nevada-school-district/?web_view=true); [The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/why-a-new-type-of-cyberattack-on-las-vegas-schools-should-worry-everyone/).
- AP investigation (2023): ransomware gangs posted kids' private files online, including records of student sexual assaults, psychiatric hospitalizations, abusive parents and suicide attempts — [PBS/AP](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/nation/after-school-hacks-ransomware-criminals-expose-kids-private-files-online).

### Inferences
- The safeguarding harm of school breaches is less "identity theft" and more **exposure of child-protection content** (abuse reports, counseling notes, custody details). Small orgs that keep incident/abuse-report files in shared drives or email should be a priority.
- Rosters with student emails/social handles + photos lower the cost of targeting; a plausible but unproven link. Defensive advice (don't publish student emails or full names with photos) is justified on precaution regardless.

### Gaps
- No primary source found showing US sextortion offenders selecting victims from leaked school rosters.
- FBI has not published a full 2025 count in results.

## Children's photos and identifying info published by schools/parishes/childcare (websites, social, newsletters, classroom livestream cameras)

### Takeaway
Published child photos are now a documented input for AI-enabled abuse and school-targeted extortion (HRW, eSafety, IWF, 2024-2026), and childcare camera/app ecosystems have a history of weak security. Treat deepfakes briefly; the broader, under-served issue is publication hygiene (names + photos + location + schedule) and the security of parent-facing camera/communication apps that fall outside COPPA.

### Cited Findings
- Human Rights Watch: identifiable children's photos in LAION-5B, some scraped from school websites and unlisted YouTube videos (2024 work; HRW Australia follow-up July 2026) — [HRW 2026](https://www.hrw.org/news/2026/07/30/australia-school-photos-scraped-for-ai-fueled-image-misuse).
- Australia eSafety: Jan–Mar 2026, >100 reports of anonymous accounts targeting schools/staff using images from official school websites and social media; guidance on sharing school imagery — [eSafety blog](https://esafety.gov.au/newsroom/blogs/sharing-school-imagery-in-the-age-of-ai-risks-deepfakes-and-how-to-protect-students-and-staff).
- UK survey (Aidos, 2026, 500 headteachers): 49% reported an incident where pupil images were misused/manipulated; only 43% said consent processes address AI risk — [search summary; Malwarebytes/related](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/family-and-parenting/2026/05/deepfake-sextortion-forces-schools-to-remove-student-photos-from-websites) (snippet truncated; vendor survey, treat cautiously).
- PimEyes-style face search can surface children's photos (2022) — [The Intercept](https://theintercept.com/2022/07/16/facial-recognition-search-children-photos-privacy-pimeyes/).
- EFF (2022): daycare apps (incl. Brightwheel, Tadpoles, HiMama) had weak practices — public access to children's photos in some apps, weak passwords, missing encryption; Brightwheel added 2FA after EFF contact; COPPA doesn't cover these adult-used apps — [EFF](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2022/06/daycare-apps-are-dangerously-insecure); [EFF 2022 review](https://www.eff.org/deeplinks/2022/12/daycare-and-early-childhood-education-apps-2022-review?language=fa).
- Ruhr-Uni Bochum study of 42 daycare apps (2022): private children's photos accessible via flaws in some apps; 40 apps tracked parents/teachers — [CACM](https://cacmb4.acm.org/news/262605-how-daycare-apps-can-spy-on-parents-children/fulltext); [SC Media](https://scmagazine.com/brief/security-flaws-prevalent-in-daycare-monitoring-apps).
- NurseryCam (UK, Feb 2021): ~12,000 accounts exposed; researcher had warned since 2015 that DVR credentials were in HTML source — [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2021/02/22/nurserycam_breach/); [Threatpost](https://threatpost.com/daycare-webcam-exposes-12000/164203/). Montreal daycare lobby camera appeared on a public webcam-streaming app — [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/2931476/montreal-residents-concerned-after-day-care-security-camera-shows-up-on-livestreaming-app).
- Recommended mitigations (UK/Australia): distant/blurred/rear-facing photos, no names in captions, reconsider posting at all — [Interactive Schools](https://www.interactiveschools.com/blog/protecting-student-photos-from-ai-misuse-online); [eSafety](https://esafety.gov.au/newsroom/blogs/sharing-school-imagery-in-the-age-of-ai-risks-deepfakes-and-how-to-protect-students-and-staff).

### Inferences
- A simple "photo publication standard" (no first+last names with faces, no uniforms/locations tied to schedules, honor custody/ACP/foster "no photo" flags, private galleries with expiring links) addresses both the targeted-individual threat and AI misuse; this framing may land better with the project owner than "deepfakes" per se.
- Childcare camera systems are an under-audited IoT risk in small centers; a volunteer can check default credentials, remote-access exposure, and vendor MFA.

### Gaps
- No 2024-2026 US daycare-camera breach found in search.
- No US data on misuse of parish newsletter/website photos.

## Who already works this space and where the gaps are for small orgs

### Takeaway
Big players cover either child exploitation response (NCMEC, Thorn, ICAC, Childhelp) or abuse-prevention compliance (VIRTUS, Praesidium, CMG, MinistrySafe), and survivor tech-safety (NNEDV Safety Net, CETA). Nobody found in this search offers hands-on, free help to a small parish, preschool or youth program to make its *systems and records* honor safeguarding needs. That integration layer is the volunteer opportunity.

### Cited Findings
- Thorn: Stop Sextortion hub, educator/parent tip sheets, NoFiltr peer program; Thorn is not a grantmaker — [Thorn FAQ](https://www.thorn.org/faqs/); [Amaze educator resources](https://amaze.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Educator-Talks_Sextortion_Resources.pdf).
- NCMEC: sextortion resources, family support, Take It Down image-removal — [ICAC fact sheet](https://www.icactaskforce.org/assets/FMS%20and%20SOE%20Fact%20Sheet_Black%20and%20White.pdf).
