## 1. FERPA: does it reach private/religious schools, childcare and pre-K?

### Takeaway
The prior claim holds and is the Department of Education's own stated position: FERPA attaches only to institutions receiving funds from programs **administered by the U.S. Department of Education**, and private/parochial K-12 schools "generally" do not, so they are generally not subject to FERPA. Equitable services under ESEA do not change this, because the funds go to the public LEA, not the private school.

### Cited Findings
- SPPO FAQ: FERPA applies to educational agencies or institutions that receive funds from programs administered by the U.S. Department of Education; "private and parochial elementary and secondary schools generally do not receive such funding and are, therefore, not subject to FERPA." Private postsecondary institutions generally do and are covered. — [studentprivacy.ed.gov FAQ (via search snippet)](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/which-educational-agencies-or-institutions-does-ferpa-apply); same in [CDC Public Health Law Program summary](https://www.cdc.gov/phlp/publications/topic/ferpa.html)
- ED states it lacks jurisdiction over private K-12 schools unless they are direct recipients of federal financial assistance from the Department. — [ED non-public education FAQ](https://www.ed.gov/birth-grade-12-education/education-choice/frequently-asked-questions-non-public-education)
- Under ESEA equitable services, private schools do not receive the funds themselves; the LEA retains control of funds and provides the services, so the private school is not treated as a recipient. — [CRS R46907](https://www.everycrsreport.com/reports/R46907.html)
- Contrary view: EPIC states "virtually all private schools are covered by FERPA because they receive some sort of federal funding." This conflicts with ED's own FAQ and appears to conflate any federal money with ED-administered funds. — [EPIC FERPA page](https://epic.org/family-educational-rights-and-privacy-act-ferpa/)
- Illinois ISBE equitable-services guidance contains a dedicated FAQ on whether private schools receiving equitable services are subject to FERPA (answer text not retrieved). — [ISBE equitable guidance](https://www.isbe.net/Documents/equitable_guidance.pdf)
- Head Start: programs must protect PII in child records under 45 CFR 1303 Subpart C; a Head Start program that is itself subject to FERPA follows FERPA instead, and IDEA confidentiality applies to children served under IDEA. — [45 CFR 1303 Subpart C (Cornell LII)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/45/part-1303/subpart-C); [Network for Public Health Law snapshot](https://networkforphl.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/01/Snapshot-Head-Start_final1.pdf)
- 45 CFR 1303.22 governs disclosures without parental consent and gives parents an opportunity to challenge disclosures to schools/districts. — [headstart.gov 1303.22](https://headstart.gov/policy/45-cfr-chap-xiii/1303-22-disclosures-without-parental-consent)

### Inferences
- **School meals (USDA NSLP) and E-Rate (FCC/USAC) are not ED-administered programs**, so participation should not by itself trigger FERPA. This is a reading of the FERPA statute's "applicable program" language, not something found stated in an ED source this session. Note NSLP has its own confidentiality rule for free/reduced-price eligibility data (42 U.S.C. 1758(b)(6)) **[training knowledge, unverified]**: a school participating in NSLP is bound by that narrower rule for meal-eligibility data.
- Exceptions where a private school may be a FERPA "educational agency or institution": direct ED grants (rare), or where a public LEA places a student with disabilities in a private school (the records the LEA maintains/controls are FERPA records). Private preschools/childcare are outside FERPA unless run by a public LEA/ED-funded entity.
- Practical consequence: for most private, parochial, classical schools and private childcare, **no federal statute grants parents access/consent rights over student records**; protection comes only from state law, contracts, and COPPA (vendor side).
- Head Start grantees (often small nonprofits) are the one childcare segment with a binding federal FERPA-like regime (1303 Subpart C), enforceable through HHS grant monitoring.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the SPPO FAQ text directly (studentprivacy.ed.gov blocked); quote is from search snippet.
- CCDF (45 CFR Part 98) confidentiality provisions not retrieved; no source found this session on what CCDF requires of subsidized private providers' records.
- State childcare licensing record-confidentiality rules not researched in detail (vary by state; no consolidated source found).

## 2. FTC's amended COPPA Rule and edtech at small schools

### Takeaway
Final (not proposed). Approved Jan 2025, published in the Federal Register April 22, 2025, effective June 23, 2025, **compliance required by April 22, 2026** (now fully enforceable). Key new duties for edtech vendors: a **written information security program**, a **written data retention policy** with no indefinite retention, written assurances from third parties, and separate consent for disclosures to third parties. The FTC **declined to codify the "school authorization" exception**, leaving it in non-binding FAQ guidance.

### Cited Findings
- Final rule published April 22, 2025; effective June 23, 2025; compliance deadline April 22, 2026, except certain provisions of 312.11 (safe harbor programs). — [Federal Register 2025-05904](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule); [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/coppa-rule-amendment-compliance-deadline-approaches); [Toy Association](https://www.toyassociation.org/PressRoom2/News/2026-News/updated-coppa-rule-requirements-take-effect-april-22.aspx)
- Operators must maintain a written data security program, including designating an employee to coordinate it, with safeguards scaled to sensitivity of data and operator size; may rely on an enterprise-wide program if it covers children's data. Must obtain written assurances from third parties receiving children's data. — [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect); [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/childrens-online-privacy-in-2025-the-amended-coppa-rule)
- Written retention policy required, stating purposes, business need and deletion timeframe, disclosed in the COPPA notice; timeframes cannot be indefinite. — [Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/childrens-online-privacy-in-2025-the-amended-coppa-rule); [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/unpacking-ftcs-coppa-amendments-what-you-need-know)
- FTC declined to codify school authorization exception, citing a potential ED student privacy rulemaking; it will enforce consistent with the COPPA FAQs. A firm notes the FTC could revoke the exception merely by revising the FAQs. — [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-FTC-Adopts-Amended-Childrens-Online-Protection-Act-Rule); [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en-gb/insights/publications/2025/01/ftc-finalizes-changes-to-coppa)
- Max COPPA civil penalty cited at $53,088 per violation. — [Davis Polk](https://www.davispolk.com/insights/client-update/ftc-prioritizes-coppa-enforcement-new-compliance-obligations-take-effect)
- Statement of basis and purpose. — [FTC SBP PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/coppa_sbp_1.16_0.pdf)

### Inferences
- COPPA binds **operators (vendors)**, not schools; it protects only under-13s. It therefore protects private-school and childcare children **indirectly** whenever the org uses an online service, regardless of FERPA status. This is the strongest federal hook for small orgs.
- Under the FAQ-based school-authorization practice, a school (including a private school) can consent on parents' behalf only for educational use. A small org that "consents" for vendors takes on a de facto duty to vet them; it has a legitimate basis to demand the vendor's written security program and retention policy, since the vendor is now required to have them. **This is a concrete procurement lever.**
- Small orgs that themselves run child-directed websites/apps (e.g., a childcare parent app built in-house, a youth program registration site collecting under-13 data directly from kids) may themselves be COPPA operators. Note: COPPA applies to "persons" operating commercially; nonprofits are generally outside FTC Act jurisdiction **[training knowledge, unverified]**, a gap for nonprofit schools/programs, though their for-profit vendors are covered.

### Gaps
- Exact regulatory section numbers (312.8, 312.10) not confirmed from rule text this session (snippets only).
- No source found on FTC COPPA enforcement actions after April 2026 specific to edtech.

## 3. FTC edtech enforcement and the Safeguards Rule

### Takeaway
FTC orders against edtech (Illuminate 2025-26, Chegg 2022, Edmodo 2023) set a de facto security baseline: written security program, MFA/access controls, data inventory, retention schedules and deletion, third-party assessments, and limits on outsourcing compliance to schools. These orders bind only the named firms but signal what the FTC treats as "reasonable." The Safeguards Rule reaches entities "significantly engaged" in financial activities; tuition-payment/financing firms plausibly qualify, SGOs that only make grants likely do not (interpretation).

### Cited Findings
- Illuminate: FTC proposed order Dec 1, 2025; alleged plaintext storage of student data until at least Jan 2022 and ignored vendor warnings; breach involved ~10M students. — [FTC press release, Dec 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/12/ftc-takes-action-against-education-technology-provider-failing-secure-students-personal-data); [NatLawReview](https://natlawreview.com/article/ftc-settles-illuminate-data-breach-10m-students-data)
- Illuminate order: comprehensive information security program; MFA and access reviews; data inventory/classification; designated overseer reporting to board annually and within 30 days of any incident; biennial third-party assessments; delete data no longer needed and follow a **public** retention schedule; bar on misrepresenting security and **breach-notification timing to districts**; notify FTC when it notifies another government entity of a breach. 10-year order. — [Covington InsidePrivacy](https://www.insideprivacy.com/united-states/federal-trade-commission/ftc-announces-10-year-information-security-consent-orders-with-illuminate-education-and-illusory-systems/); [GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/education/k-12/ftc-orders-illuminate-to-improve-security-limit-data-retention); [FTC Decision and Order PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/IlluminateEducationDecisionandOrder.pdf)
- Final Illuminate order (June 2026) added one substantive change after public comment: explicit data-minimization requirement, following EPIC's comment. — [EPIC](https://epic.org/ftc-finalizes-settlement-with-illuminate-education-heeding-epics-call-to-strengthen-data-minimization-requirements/) **(Lever proof: a public comment on a consent order changed final terms.)**
- Edmodo (2023): FTC alleged unlawful use of children's data for advertising and that Edmodo outsourced COPPA compliance to schools; order prohibited conditioning use on collecting more data than necessary and barred ad use of student data. — [FTC press release May 2023](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2023/05/ftc-says-ed-tech-provider-edmodo-unlawfully-used-childrens-personal-information-advertising-outsourced-compliance-school) **[URL and details from training knowledge, unverified this session]**
- Chegg (2022): order required data security program, MFA, data minimization and a retention schedule after repeated breaches. — [FTC press release Oct 2022](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/10/ftc-brings-action-against-ed-tech-provider-chegg-careless-security-exposed-personal-data-millions-customers) **[training knowledge, unverified this session]**
- Safeguards Rule: "financial institution" includes many businesses that don't call themselves that; 314.2(h) lists four examples of non-financial institutions; 2021 amendments added entities engaged in activities "incidental to financial activities" (finders). — [FTC business guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/safeguards-rule-what-your-business-needs-know); [Federal Register 2021-25736](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2021/12/09/2021-25736/standards-for-safeguarding-customer-information)
- Title IV colleges, including nonprofits, must comply with GLBA safeguards (via ED's program participation agreements). — [EDUCAUSE](https://er.educause.edu/articles/2021/12/policy-analysis-revised-highly-prescriptive-ftc-safeguards-rule)
- 2023 amendment added FTC notification for security events involving unencrypted info of 500+ consumers. — [Federal Register Nov 13, 2023](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2023-11-13/html/2023-24412.htm)

### Inferences
- **Tuition-management/payment-plan firms** (for-profit, collecting bank/card data, administering installment plans, and in many cases performing financial-need assessment with tax documents) are strong candidates to be Safeguards Rule "financial institutions." That brings mandatory written program, a "qualified individual," MFA, encryption, and FTC breach reporting. This is interpretation; no FTC statement on tuition firms was found.
- **SGOs** are 501(c)(3)s, generally outside FTC Act jurisdiction, and grant-making is not lending. Likely not covered. Gap.
- Volunteers can cite Illuminate order terms as a "reasonable security" benchmark in vendor questionnaires and contracts (e.g., public retention schedule, breach notice to the school within X days).

### Gaps
- Edmodo and Chegg details not re-verified this session.
- No FTC guidance found applying Safeguards Rule to K-12 tuition management or financial-aid assessment vendors.

## 4. State laws: which reach private schools, childcare, vendors

### Takeaway
State student-privacy law splits into two models. **School-obligation laws** (NY Ed Law 2-d; Illinois SOPPA's school duties) mostly target public schools. **Operator laws** (California SOPIPA and its many copies) bind K-12 vendors **regardless of whether the school is public or private**, which is the main route by which state law protects private-school students. Kids-code / age-appropriate design laws are in active litigation; NY's Child Data Protection Act is in effect.

### Cited Findings
- NY Ed Law 2-d / Part 121: "educational agency" means a school district, BOCES, school, or the State Education Department. — [NYSED 2-d definitions](https://www.nysed.gov/data-privacy-security/education-law-section-2-d-definitions); [8 NYCRR 121.1](https://regulations.justia.com/states/new-york/title-8/chapter-ii/subchapter-e/part-121/section-121-1/)
- **[Training knowledge, unverified]** 2-d defines "school" to mean public schools including charters, universal pre-K programs (Ed Law 3602-e), approved preschool special-education providers, other publicly funded pre-K, approved private schools for students with disabilities, and certain state-supported schools. If accurate, ordinary private/parochial schools are excluded, but **community-based childcare providers running NY UPK and "853" special-ed private schools are covered** for those programs. Verify against Ed Law 2-d(1).
- NY Child Data Protection Act (GBL 899-ee et seq.): effective June 20, 2025; applies to operators with actual knowledge a user is a minor or whose service is primarily directed to minors; covers under-18s; processing of 13-17 data only if strictly necessary or with informed consent; AG-only enforcement, up to $5,000 per violation; AG rulemaking pending (ANPRM Aug 1, 2024). — [NY AG guidance](https://ag.ny.gov/child-data-protection-act-guidance); [Goodwin](https://www.goodwinlaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/06/alerts-practices-dpc-new-yorks-child-data-protection-act-now-effect); [MoFo](https://www.mofo.com/resources/insights/250701-the-new-york-child-data-protection-act)
- Illinois SOPPA regulates student data held by schools, ISBE, and edtech operators; school-side obligations described as applying to public schools; 2019/2021 amendments added breach notification. — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/privacy/updated-illinois-student-privacy-compliance-soppa/); [Lewis Brisbois](https://lewisbrisbois.com/print/15888)
- California AADC: Ninth Circuit (Mar 12, 2026) vacated the broad facial injunction but kept injunction on certain data-use restrictions and dark-pattern provisions; mandate issued Apr 3, 2026, so many provisions now in effect; remanded on age estimation/severability. — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2026/2026-03-30-netchoice-v-bonta-ninth-circuit-narrows-injunction-against-californias-ageappropriate-design-code-act); [Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-Injunction-on-California-AADC-Partially-Vacated-Key-Provisions-May-Take-Effect-on-April-2); [Hintze](https://hintzelaw.com/blog/2026/03/17/9th-circuit-rules-on-injunction-of-california-age-appropriate-design-code-upholding-coverage-definition/)
- Texas SCOPE Act (HB 18): monitoring/filtering provisions preliminarily enjoined (CCIA/NetChoice); further provisions enjoined Feb 2025 (FIRE case); Fifth Circuit affirmed in part (Section 230 preemption); some provisions remain in effect. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/fifth-circuit-keeps-texas-online-safety-law-partially-blocked); [Route Fifty](https://www.route-fifty.com/digital-government/2025/02/texas-social-media-law-takes-another-hit-temporary-blocks-three-more-provisions/402861/)
- Maryland Kids Code: NetChoice v. Brown survived motion to dismiss (Nov 2025); law remains in effect pending litigation. — [D. Md. opinion](https://www2.mdd.uscourts.gov/Opinions/Opinions/Memorandum%20Opinion_NetChoice%20v.%20Brown%20(RDB-25-0322).pdf); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/litigation/netchoice-advances-free-speech-challenge-to-maryland-kids-code)
- **[Training knowledge, unverified]** California SOPIPA (Bus. & Prof. Code 22584) applies to operators of sites/services used primarily for K-12 school purposes and designed and marketed as such; bars targeted ads, profiling and sale; requires reasonable security and deletion on school request. It does not depend on public/private status of the school.

### Inferences
- For a private school or childcare center in a SOPIPA-style state, the vendor's statutory duties exist even though the school has no FERPA/2-d duties; the school can invoke them in contracts and complaints to the AG.
- Design-code and CDPA-type laws target online services, not schools; they matter for youth-program apps and edtech, rarely for the small org itself.
- Status as of late 2026: AADC partially in effect (litigation ongoing); SCOPE partially enjoined; Maryland Kids Code in effect but challenged; NY CDPA in effect, rules not yet final (no source confirming final rules).

### Gaps
- SOPPA's statutory definition of "school" (whether nonpublic schools are covered) could not be read; ilga.gov blocked. Must verify 105 ILCS 85/5.
- Connecticut (2023 minors' online-privacy amendments to CTDPA) and Texas SCOPE's school-carveout not researched.
- No consolidated list found of state student-privacy laws that expressly cover private schools.

## 5. Breach notification and minors; child credit freezes

### Takeaway
Child-specific credit freezes are settled federal law (since Sept 21, 2018, free freezes for under-16s via a parent). No source was found showing any state breach law with minor-specific notice requirements; general breach laws apply to whatever "personal information" (usually SSN, financial account, etc.) a small org holds, subject to each state's applicability.

### Cited Findings
- Under S. 2155 (Economic Growth, Regulatory Relief, and Consumer Protection Act), since Sept 21, 2018 parents can place free credit freezes for children under 16 in all states. — [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/new-protections-available-minors-under-16); [FTC consumer advice](https://consumer.ftc.gov/node/298282)
- Prior state "protected consumer" freeze laws covered minors under 16 or 18 and persons with guardians; 33 states by Aug 2018. — [ITRC info sheet](https://idtheftcenter.org/?p=7388); [CA AG](https://oag.ca.gov/idtheft/facts/freeze-child-credit)
- Illinois SOPPA added breach notification duties for schools and operators. — [Lewis Brisbois](https://lewisbrisbois.com/print/15888)

### Inferences
- A practical post-breach lever: small orgs that lose SSNs/birth dates of children should recommend (and fund via insurance) child credit freezes, which are more protective than adult-style credit monitoring because children have no files to monitor.

### Gaps
- No state found with minor-specific breach notice terms; did not complete a 50-state check.

## 6. Nonprofit and religious exemptions in comprehensive state privacy laws

### Takeaway
Most comprehensive state privacy laws exempt nonprofits, but Colorado, Oregon, New Jersey, Delaware, Maryland and Minnesota generally do not (with narrow carve-outs). In practice the bigger gap is **volume thresholds**: a single parish school or childcare center will almost never meet consumer-count thresholds, so even in non-exempting states small religious orgs fall outside. No source found addressing religious-specific exemptions.

### Cited Findings
- CO, DE, MD, MN, NJ, OR apply generally to nonprofits, with limited carve-outs, if thresholds are met. — [Wiley](https://wiley.law/alert-New-State-Privacy-Laws-May-Apply-to-Nonprofit-Organizations); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2023/10/what-nonprofits-need-to-know-about-state)
- Conflict: Delaware described both as not broadly exempting nonprofits and as exempting only certain mission nonprofits; Minnesota described as no exemption vs. exemption only for insurance-fraud nonprofits. — [Wagenmaker & Oberly](https://wagenmakerlaw.com/blog/new-state-data-privacy-laws-affect-nonprofits); [Independent Sector](https://independentsector.org/blog/fashionable-legislation-how-states-are-taking-charge-of-data-privacy-and-what-it-means-for-nonprofits/)
- State comparison chart. — [Troutman chart Jan 2025](https://www.troutman.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/U.S.-State-Comprehensive-Privacy-Laws-Chart-January-2025-revised-2.pdf); [IAPP tracker](https://iapp.org/resources/article/us-state-privacy-laws-overview)

### Inferences
- A Catholic diocese or a large SGO/school-choice platform aggregating many families might cross thresholds in a non-exempting state; individual parishes/schools won't. Dioceses that centralize SIS/donor data are the realistic target for "you may be covered" outreach.
- Nonprofits are also generally outside FTC Act Section 5 jurisdiction (training knowledge), so neither the FTC nor most state comprehensive laws reach a typical parish school. The remaining hooks are state breach laws (which typically apply to any entity holding PI) and state UDAP laws (scope varies).

### Gaps
- Religious-organization-specific exemptions not found in any source.
- Thresholds for each state not compiled here.

## 7. SGOs, school-choice platforms, and tax data (IRC 6103 and the new federal credit)

### Takeaway
The prior claim is correct as a matter of statutory structure: IRC 6103 restricts disclosure by government officers/employees, not by private recipients of a return a family hands over (interpretation; statute text not retrieved). **New and time-sensitive:** Treasury/IRS issued temporary and proposed regulations for the federal scholarship tax credit (IRC 25F) on Oct 1-2, 2026; the proposal lets SGOs verify income via **tax returns, W-2s, pay stubs, or IRS transcripts**; **comments due Dec 1, 2026; public hearing Dec 15, 2026.** This is the single best near-term lever.

### Cited Findings
- Federal Scholarship Tax Credit (IRC 25F): up to $1,700 per individual credit for contributions to SGOs; for contributions on or after Jan 1, 2027; only in states that opt in; SGOs must be 501(c)(3) public charities on a state list and meet 25F(c)(5). — [IRS FSTC page](https://www.irs.gov/government-entities/federal-state-local-governments/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-fstc); [NCSL](https://www.ncsl.org/resources/details/4-questions-about-the-new-federal-tax-credit-scholarship); [CLA](https://www.claconnect.com/en/resources/blogs/nonprofits/scholarship-granting-organizations-and-the-one-big-beautiful-bill-act)
- Temporary and proposed regulations published Oct 2026 (FR docs 2026-20277 and 2026-20264). — [Federal Register 2026-20277](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20277/federal-scholarship-tax-credit); [public inspection PDF](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-20277.pdf); [EdWeek](https://edweek.org/policy-politics/federal-education-tax-credit-rules-are-finally-here-what-we-know-so-far/2026/10)
- 60-day comment period, comments due Dec 1, 2026; hearing Dec 15, 2026. Income verification via direct documentation (pay stubs, tax returns, W-2s, IRS transcripts) or categorical eligibility (SNAP, TANF, WIC, Section 8, SSI within 12 months); foster children deemed eligible. — [Brownstein Q&A](https://www.bhfs.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Federal-Scholarship-Tax-Credit-QA-102026_VFINAL.pdf); [Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/10/treasury-department-guidance-clarifies-the-education); [Nelson Mullins](https://www.nelsonmullins.com/storage/FyiXqGKZuS2oQPQhoHlpJ2KydkC5SUPPF967Glra.pdf)
- Advocacy groups have already filed joint comments on the program (earlier comment request, Dec 2025). — [EdTrust](https://edtrust.org/press-room/joint-public-comment-on-the-federal-tax-credit-program-for-contributions-to-scholarship-granting-organizations/); [NYSSCPA](https://www.nysscpa.org/article-content/irs-requests-comment-on-new-scholarship-tax-credit-regulations-12012025)

### Inferences
- IRC 6103 binds federal/state officials; IRC 7216 binds tax return preparers. Neither reaches an SGO or tuition-assessment vendor that receives a 1040 from a parent. This is the researcher's reading of the statutes, consistent with the prior analysis; not confirmed by a cited source this session.
- The proposed regs (per snippets) do not appear to include data security, minimization or retention rules for applicant documents; not confirmed because full text was unreadable. **Comment ask:** prefer categorical eligibility and IRS transcript/verification-only methods over full returns; require SGOs to minimize (retain eligibility determination, not the documents), set retention limits, and adopt a written security program; and ask Treasury to tie SGO certification to these. States that opt in also set their SGO lists, so **state opt-in legislation/rules (2027 sessions)** are a second venue.

### Gaps
- Full text of proposed SGO provisions (any confidentiality/recordkeeping language) not read; federalregister.gov blocked.
- No source checked on state tax-credit scholarship program data rules (e.g., FL, AZ, PA).

## 8. Levers a volunteer advocate can realistically use

### Takeaway
Ranked by realism: (1) file comments on the 25F SGO regulations by **Dec 1, 2026** and appear at the Dec 15 hearing; (2) use COPPA's now-mandatory vendor duties (written security program, retention policy) and SOPIPA-style statutes as procurement demands, packaged via the SDPC NDPA v2 template; (3) cite FTC orders (Illuminate) as the reasonableness benchmark; (4) file targeted complaints with the FTC (for-profit vendors) and state AGs (NY CDPA, SOPIPA states); (5) comment on FTC consent orders, which has demonstrably changed outcomes.

### Cited Findings
- SDPC/A4L National Data Privacy Agreement v2 (usage guide v2.4, April 2025) with state-specific Exhibit G incorporated by checkbox; standard web copy marked "not for use" (must be executed via SDPC). — [NDPA v2 usage guide](https://files.a4l.org/privacy/NDPA/NDPAv2_Usage_Guide_and_Development_Process.pdf); [NDPA v2 key changes](https://a4l.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/103000315808-ndpa-v2-key-changes-from-ndpa-v1); [NDPA v2.2 standard](https://files.a4l.org/privacy/NDPA/NDPA_v2-2_STANDARD_WEB.pdf); [FPF launch post](https://fpf.org/blog/the-first-national-model-student-data-privacy-agreement-launches/)

### Inferences
- NDPA is drafted for public LEAs; private schools can still sign it as the "LEA" party in practice (no source confirms SDPC policy). Its value for small orgs is as a free, vendor-recognized template; volunteers can supply a short private-school addendum (retention, breach notice to school within 72 hours, written security program per COPPA 312.8).
- Insurance: cyber policies increasingly condition coverage on MFA/backups; no source gathered this session. Head Start (HHS monitoring), NY UPK providers (2-d via district contracts), and state-voucher/ESA platforms (state contracts) are places where **public-funding strings** can carry security terms; procurement terms in state ESA vendor contracts are a lever worth researching.
- The structural gap to articulate: a typical parochial school or nonprofit childcare center is outside FERPA, outside COPPA/FTC (as a nonprofit, and not an operator), outside most state comprehensive laws (nonprofit exemption or thresholds), and outside school-obligation student-privacy statutes (public-only). Its only binding duties are state breach-notification law, any licensing record rules, NSLP eligibility confidentiality (if participating), and contracts. Its vendors, by contrast, are covered by COPPA and SOPIPA-style laws.
