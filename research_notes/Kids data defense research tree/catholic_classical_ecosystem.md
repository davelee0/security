## 1. Scale: Catholic schools, classical schools, growth and conversions

### Takeaway
About 5,800 US Catholic schools enroll about 1.67 million students (NCEA 2025-26). Enrollment has held roughly flat since a post-COVID bump, but it is down about 70% from its 1964 peak. The classical sector is smaller and fragmented but growing fast: more than 1,000 schools, about 250 of them opened since 2020. Catholic classical conversions (ICLE, about 237 members) are a growing bridge between the two worlds.

### Cited Findings
- NCEA 2025-26: 1,674,907 students in 5,829 schools (4,644 elementary/middle, 1,185 secondary); 151,514 professional staff. NCEA described enrollment as "steady" with growing diversity (press release March 24, 2026). — [NCEA press release](https://ncea.org/NCEA/How_We_Serve/News/Press_Releases/National-Data-Highlights-Strong-Demand-and-Growing-Diversity-and-Inclusion-in-Catholic-Schools.aspx); [NCEA 2025-26 infographic](https://ncea.org/common/Uploaded%20files/How%20We%20Serve/Catholic%20School%20Data/2025-26-NCEA-Infographic.pdf)
- 2024-25: about 1.6M students, more than 150,000 staff, a student-teacher ratio of about 11:1. — [NCEA April 16, 2025](https://ncea.org/NCEA/How_We_Serve/News/Press_Releases/Catholic-Schools-by-the-Numbers-New-Data-Reveals-Trends-in-Enrollment-Staffing-and-Demographics.aspx)
- 19.4% of Catholic school students use school-choice programs. Florida Catholic enrollment rose for a 5th straight year. — [Step Up For Students](https://go.stepupforstudents.org/current-events/more-bright-spots-for-catholic-schools-in-america); [CNA/EWTN](https://ewtnnews.com/world/us-catholic-schools-report-indicates-school-choice-programs-on-the-rise)

- Classical schools: "more than 1,000 such schools throughout the U.S., over 250 of which have opened since 2020" (Heritage Foundation, Aug 2024, with a classical-school database). — [Heritage](https://www.heritage.org/education/report/classical-schools-america-movement-hope)
- An Arcadia Education analysis of ACCS, ICLE and SCL member schools found 264 new classical schools from 2019 to 2023, about 4.8% growth per year. - An AP investigation (Oct 2026) reports taxpayer-funded voucher/ESA growth is fueling classical Christian school expansion. — [AJC/AP takeaways](https://www.ajc.com/news/2026/10/takeaways-from-aps-report-on-the-taxpayer-funded-growth-of-classical-christian-schools/)
- Great Hearts: about 50 schools and 30,000+ students (mid-2025); 52 schools in 3 states (Feb 2026); 31,000+ students in 2026-27 (Wikipedia). — [Great Hearts "A New Kind of Growth"](https://www.greatheartsamerica.org/a-new-kind-of-growth-the-same-spirit-of-service/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Hearts_Academies)

- Diocese-wide conversions: Diocese of Marquette, MI, was the first diocese to move all of its schools to a classical Catholic model (around 2020). — [CNA/EWTN "ditching common core"](https://www.ewtnnews.com/world/us/why-this-diocese-is-ditching-common-core-for-liberal-arts)

### Inferences
- In absolute numbers, Catholic schools (about 5,800 sites, about 1.67M children) are by far the larger target and lever. Classical schools are numerous but small. Many are new startups with little IT staff, which suggests high risk per school.
- New voucher/ESA money brings new state-reporting and payment data flows (ESA platforms such as ClassWallet or Step Up portals). That likely raises the volume of sensitive family financial data held by small schools.

### Gaps
- No current (2025-26) official counts for ACCS, SCL, Hillsdale/BCSI member schools, or Hillsdale's broader K-12 "member school" program.
- Total US parishes and dioceses are not re-verified here. The commonly cited figures are about 16,000 to 17,000 parishes and about 195 dioceses/eparchies (CARA), but no source was retrieved in this session.
- No national count of Catholic schools converted to classical. ICLE membership is the best proxy.

## 2. Diocesan IT structures: shared tenants, SIS, central IT, policies

### Takeaway
The dominant model is federated. The diocese "consults," but parishes and schools are legally and operationally responsible for their own IT. Insurance-driven mandates (MFA, EDR, training) are increasingly the de facto central security policy. A few dioceses, notably Seattle, now publish mandatory digital security baselines. Central school offices often run a common SIS (PowerSchool or FACTS), which concentrates student data at one vendor per diocese.

### Cited Findings
- Archdiocese of Seattle "AOS Guardian": every parish, school and ministry must implement AOS Guardian or an approved equivalent. A mandate summary dated February 2026 says Microsoft 365's built-in security is not sufficient on its own and covers conditional access. Seattle also issued a 2025 warning about phishing and token theft targeting parishes. — [Guardian Mandate PDF](https://archseattle.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Guardian-Mandate-Final.pdf); [Seattle IT page](https://archseattle.org/for-parishes/information-technology/) (full text not fetchable from this session)
- A Gallagher convocation workshop (2022) on the "diocesan enterprise" describes the typical model: diocesan IT "consults" with parishes and schools, "but they are ultimately responsible for their own IT." It recommends MFA for M365. — [Gallagher PDF](https://www.ajg.com/us/-/media/files/gallagher/us/events/convocation-2022-workshops/coming-together-to-achieve-the-right-cyber-coverage-for-the-diocesan-enterprise.pdf)
- Diocese of Metuchen rolled out MFA for diocesan Microsoft 365 users starting 01/25/2023. This implies a diocesan M365 tenant. — [search summary of Metuchen help/MFA form](https://form.jotform.com/230176026724047)
- Archdiocese of Atlanta MFA policy for churches and schools: Microsoft Authenticator is the preferred method; SMS/email only as backup. — [Atlanta MFA policy PDF](https://communique.archatl.com/wp-content/uploads/Multi-factor-authentication-MFA-policy.pdf); [resources.archatl.com](https://resources.archatl.com/?p=6403)
- Archdiocese of Milwaukee "Mandate: Cyber Security" (Sept 2022): mandatory staff cyber training via Catholic Mutual's CMGConnect by Dec 31, 2022. A Nov 2024 CMG document notes that lacking MFA on M365 raises takeover risk. — [MandateCyberSecurity2022.09.06.pdf](https://www.archmil.org/ArchMil/Resources/FIN/MandateCyberSecurity2022.09.06.pdf); [202411CatholicMutualGroup.pdf](https://archmil.org/ArchMil/Resources/ParFin/202411CatholicMutualGroup.pdf); [NAS Cyber Triage Guidance](https://www.archmil.org/ArchMil/Resources/FIN/NAS-CyberTriageGuidance-CMG-MilweditsParishes.pdf)
- Archdiocese of St. Paul & Minneapolis published MFA requirements for parishes and schools. — [missionsupport.archspm.org](https://missionsupport.archspm.org/news/multi-factor-authentication-mfa-requirements/)
- A Diocese of Des Moines cyber-liability deck says that without email MFA, "insurance may not cover the loss." EDR is "Required by our insurance," and 27 locations reported not having it. Des Moines also publishes its 2025 cyber-liability renewal application. — [Cyber Liability PPT](https://www.dmdiocese.org/filesimages/Finance/Business%20Office%20Forums/Cyber%20Liability%20PPT.pdf); [2025 renewal application](https://dmdiocese.org/filesimages/Finance/Business%20Office%20Forums/2025%20Cyberliability%20renewal%20application.pdf)
- Archdiocese of Cincinnati risk page says a few parishes and schools were hit by ransomware that locked network files and disabled systems for several weeks (undated). — [Cincinnati cyber page](https://resources.catholicaoc.org/offices/finance/risk-management/cyber-security)
- Archdiocese of Detroit runs a cybersecurity and cyber liability insurance page; it notes requirements and costs have risen significantly. — [aod.org/cyber-security](https://aod.org/cyber-security)
- Diocese of Wilmington (?) "Acceptable Use of Technology in Ministry" policy (Oct 2024). — [cdow.org PDF](https://cdow.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Technology-in-Ministry-Policy.pdf)
- Archdiocese of New York's Catholic Schools office lists a central "Director, Student Information Systems" and a Technology and Data Support Liaison. That suggests centralized SIS management; the platform is not named. — [catholicschoolsny.org/contact](https://catholicschoolsny.org/contact/)
- Archdiocese of Chicago appears to use PowerSchool, per a testing vendor's onboarding article. This is indirect evidence. — [Sadlier Connect support](https://support.sadlierconnect.com/article/435-archdiocese-of-chicago)
- Archdiocese of Baltimore Department of Catholic Schools and Diocese of Oakland schools ran PowerSchool and were affected by the Dec 2024 breach (see section 4). — [archbalt.org](https://www.archbalt.org/powerschool-cybersecurity-incident-department-of-catholic-schools/); [csdo.org](https://csdo.org/powerschool-cybersecurity-incident)
- Diocese of Baton Rouge (?) Catholic schools publish FACTS SIS MFA login flyers. This suggests diocese-wide FACTS SIS adoption. — [catholicschoolsbq.org PDF (Aug 2026)](https://catholicschoolsbq.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/5-FACTS-SIS-Proceso-de-inicio-de-sesion-con.pdf)

### Inferences
- The leverage points are (a) the diocesan risk/finance office, which controls insurance renewal questionnaires, and (b) the Office of Catholic Schools technology lead, who typically picks the SIS for every school. One conversation with either can reach dozens to 200+ sites.
- Seattle's Guardian mandate, plus the Atlanta, Milwaukee, St. Paul and Des Moines documents, form a ready-made corpus of "peer" policies. A volunteer project could curate these into a template rather than writing from scratch.
- Whether a diocese uses one shared M365/Google tenant varies. Metuchen and Atlanta suggest diocesan tenants; many dioceses leave parishes on their own consumer or nonprofit tenants. The difference matters: shared tenants make MFA enforcement and conditional access a single switch.

### Gaps
- No direct confirmation of shared Google Workspace/M365 tenants at Archdiocese of New York, Brooklyn, Chicago, or Washington. Their technology pages were not found or fetched.
- No published security policy found for the Archdiocese of Washington or the Diocese of Brooklyn.
- The diocese names marked (?) above (cdow.org, catholicschoolsbq.org) were inferred from domain names and not verified.

## 3. Vendors concentrated in Catholic/private schools and parishes

### Takeaway
A small set of vendors holds most Catholic-school and parish data: FACTS (Nelnet) for tuition, aid and SIS; PowerSchool for SIS in large dioceses; Blackbaud for development and some school SIS/LMS; and Ministry Brands (ParishSOFT, WeShare, Clover Sites) for parish registries and giving. Flocknote and OSV Online Giving are also widely used. FACTS has been phasing in MFA: tuition/aid families from Nov 2023, SIS from early 2024, with some schools reporting mandatory dates as late as April 2026. No breach was found for ParishSOFT, Flocknote or WeShare. The concentrated-vendor incidents that actually hit Catholic orgs were Blackbaud (2020) and PowerSchool (2024).

### Cited Findings
- FACTS MFA: FACTS announced MFA for family users of tuition/aid products starting November 2023, and said FACTS SIS would begin requiring MFA in early 2024. — [FACTS MFA page](https://factsmgt.com/?p=136832)
- School notices show FACTS SIS MFA becoming mandatory for all non-student users on April 29, 2026 at some schools. Options are email code or any authenticator app; students are exempt from MFA on the Family Portal. — [Andrean HS notice](https://www.andreanhs.org/important-update-multi-factor-authentication-required-for-facts-access/); [Immaculata School FACTS MFA](https://www.immaculataschool.org/parents-a-z/facts-mfa)
- Nelnet (FACTS' parent) had a 2022 breach at Nelnet Servicing (student loans): 2,501,324 accounts with names, addresses and SSNs exposed, June to July 22, 2022. No FACTS involvement was reported. — [SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/25-million-impacted-data-breach-nelnet-servicing/); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2022/08/30/data-breach-nelnet-exposes-2-5-million-student-loan-records/)
- Blackbaud 2020 ransomware (began May, disclosed July 16, 2020) hit many Catholic orgs. Notices came from the Archdiocese of Atlanta, Archdiocese of Denver, Archdiocese of Los Angeles, Diocese of Des Moines, Archdiocese of Oklahoma City (on behalf of affiliated ministries), Catholic Charities Twin Cities, Diocese of Portland (ME), Catholic University of America, Catholic Medical Center, Bishop Moore Catholic HS, Light of Christ Catholic Schools (MT; SSNs), and St. Joseph School. Data included names, addresses, DOB, giving history, and some SSNs. — [archden.org](https://archden.org/statement-on-vendor-security-incident/); [archatl.com](https://archatl.com/?p=32472); [lacatholics.org/blackbaud](https://lacatholics.org/blackbaud/); [dmdiocese.org](https://www.dmdiocese.org/news/news-blackbaud-data-security-incident); [archokc.org](https://archokc.org/news/blackbaud-breach-affects-archdiocese); [cctwincities.org](https://www.cctwincities.org/notice-of-data-breach/); [Sun Journal (Portland)](https://www.sunjournal.com/2020/08/01/diocese-of-portland-information-stolen-in-cyberattack/); [CUA](https://engage.catholic.edu/stories/security-incident-blackbaud-2020); [Montana DOJ – Light of Christ](https://dojmt.gov/wp-content/uploads/Light-of-Christ-Catholic-Schools-Ad-SSN-Only-Updated-r2prf.pdf); [Maine AG – Bishop Moore](https://maine.gov/ag/attachments/985235c7-cb95-4be2-8792-a1252b4f8318/89420c42-5d9e-41c6-af81-b1bb55a91396/7cc31d8a-91c6-4300-9e09-b7e44941498f/Bishop%20Moore%20Catholic%20HS%20Sample%20Letter.pdf)
- Ministry Brands owns ParishSOFT and acquired WeShare/WeGather from LPi (Aug 5, 2021). ParishSOFT advertises multiple arch/diocesan-wide partnerships (one announcement covers twelve arch/dioceses). — [Ministry Brands acquires WeShare](https://www.ministrybrands.com/news/ministry-brands-acquires-weshare-and-wegather-assets-from-lpi); [ParishSOFT partnerships](https://parishsoft.com/?p=1366)
- Clover Sites (Ministry Brands church-website brand) is the subject of a WebsitePlanet "leak report." Details could not be verified from the snippet. — [WebsitePlanet](https://www.websiteplanet.com/blog/cloversites-leak-report)
- Flocknote is a widely used parish communications platform. Its founder, Matthew Warner, has spoken publicly (OSV News 2023) about parish cyber weakness. — [Catholic Review/OSV News](https://catholicreview.org/parish-cybersecurity-at-risk-but-theres-hope-say-experts/)
- IT-provider supply chain: St. Mary's Catholic High School (MA) was breached through its IT services vendor Veeya (MA breach #2024-534). — [mass.gov](https://www.mass.gov/doc/assigned-data-breach-number-2024-534-st-marys-catholic-high-school/download)

### Inferences
- FACTS is the single highest-leverage vendor. It serves thousands of Catholic and private schools for tuition, financial aid (including family tax and income documents) and SIS. Its MFA rollout includes email-code fallback, which is weaker against mailbox compromise. Encouraging schools to turn on staff/admin MFA and authenticator apps is a cheap, scalable ask.
- Because diocesan-wide ParishSOFT contracts exist, a diocese-level configuration change (MFA or SSO) could reach every parish registry in that diocese at once.
- Vendor concentration means breach notifications arrive as waves (Blackbaud, PowerSchool). A pre-written "vendor breach response kit" for school and parish leaders would be reusable.

### Gaps
- No public evidence found of breaches at FACTS, ParishSOFT, Flocknote, WeShare, OSV Online Giving, Sycamore, Gradelink or Veracross. Their MFA enforcement status (beyond FACTS) was not determined.
- No market-share figures were found for FACTS vs. PowerSchool vs. Blackbaud among Catholic schools.

## 4. Documented cyber incidents (2020-2026)

### Takeaway
Incidents cluster in three forms: (1) third-party vendor breaches (Blackbaud 2020, PowerSchool Dec 2024, Veeya 2024, and bankruptcy-advisor breaches at BRG and Stinson in 2025); (2) direct ransomware and network intrusions at Catholic Charities affiliates (San Francisco 2024, Southern Nevada 2025, Albany 2025) and some parishes/schools (Cincinnati, Ancillae-Assumpta 2023); and (3) BEC/wire fraud at parishes, the canonical case being St. Ambrose, Brunswick OH, about $1.75M in 2019. The 2025 bankruptcy-advisor breaches are especially sensitive because they exposed abuse-survivor data.

### Cited Findings
- PowerSchool (Dec 19-28, 2024; compromised credential): Archdiocese of Baltimore Catholic schools' data stored in PowerSchool was affected; archdiocesan systems were not breached; PowerSchool sent the notifications. The Diocese of Oakland schools were also affected. — [archbalt.org notice](https://www.archbalt.org/important-notice-nationwide-powerschool-data-breach-impacting-archdiocese-of-baltimore-catholic-schools/); [csdo.org](https://csdo.org/powerschool-cybersecurity-incident)
- Berkeley Research Group (BRG) ransomware (2025): BRG advised creditors in diocesan bankruptcies; it paid the actor to delete data, per filings in at least eight Catholic diocese Chapter 11 cases. The Archdiocese of New Orleans said accounts-payable data, including information on employees, teachers, students and parents in the Catholic school system and abuse survivors, may have been accessed (May 2025). — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/bankrupt-catholic-groups-data-stolen-in-brg-ransomware-attack); [Fox8 New Orleans, May 5, 2025](https://www.fox8live.com/2025/05/05/information-sex-abuse-survivors-archdiocese-no-possibly-compromised-ransomware-attack/)
- Stinson LLP (creditors' counsel in the Baltimore archdiocese bankruptcy) was breached in Feb 2025 and disclosed it in June 2025. — [BishopAccountability, Aug 2025](https://www.bishop-accountability.org/2025/08/another-cyber-breach-threatens-baltimore-catholic-church-abuse-survivors/)
- Catholic Charities of the Diocese of Albany was claimed by the Pear ransomware group (attack est. May 16, 2025; posted Sept 2025; about 200GB claimed, unverified). — [ransomware.live](https://www.ransomware.live/id/Q2F0aG9saWMgQ2hhcml0aWVzIG9mIHRoZSBEaW9jZXNlIG9mIEFsYmFueUBwZWFy); [HookPhish](https://www.hookphish.com/blog/ransomware-group-pear-hits-catholic-charities-of-the-diocese-of-albany/)
- Catholic Charities of Southern Nevada issued a notice of data event (Feb 7, 2025). — [catholiccharities.com](https://www.catholiccharities.com/notice-of-data-event)

- A ransomware.live entry exists for ccrcda.org (likely Catholic Charities of the Roman Catholic Diocese of Albany; unverified duplicate). — [ransomware.live](https://m.ransomware.live/id/Y2NyY2RhLm9yZ0BpbmNyYW5zb20)
- St. Mary's Catholic High School (MA) was breached via IT vendor Veeya (2024). — [mass.gov #2024-534](https://www.mass.gov/doc/assigned-data-breach-number-2024-534-st-marys-catholic-high-school/download)
- Ancillae-Assumpta Academy (Wyncote, PA) was claimed by the Snatch ransomware group, Oct 2023 (per search summary; primary source not retrieved).
- Archdiocese of Cincinnati: parishes and schools were hit by ransomware with systems disabled for weeks (undated). — [catholicaoc.org](https://resources.catholicaoc.org/offices/finance/risk-management/cyber-security)
- BEC: St. Ambrose Parish (Brunswick, OH, Diocese of Cleveland) lost about $1.75M (April 2019). Two staff email accounts were compromised and used to spoof the construction contractor's bank change. Outside the 2020+ window but the archetype. — [SC Media](https://www.scmagazine.com/news/st-ambrose-catholic-parish-in-brunswick-ohio-was-hit-with-a-business-email-compromise-scam); [Infosecurity](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/us-church-hit-in-18m-bec-scam-1/)
- Archdiocese of Seattle personal-info breach surfaced via fraudulent tax returns (older, ~2015; NCR). — [NCR](https://www.ncronline.org/news/parish/personal-info-security-breach-stings-seattle-archdiocese)
- 2025 St. Paul (city) cyberattack: municipal, not Catholic. Listed for disambiguation only. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_St._Paul_cyberattack)
- UK context (Aug 2025): a third-party breach affected at least 10 Church of England dioceses' parishioner data. — [Church Times](https://www.churchtimes.co.uk/articles/2025/29-august/news/uk/cyber-attack-leaves-hundreds-of-parishioners-at-risk-of-identity-theft)

### Inferences
- Parish BEC losses are probably heavily under-reported. Parishes are not subject to SEC or most breach laws for wire fraud (no PII), and dioceses settle quietly via Catholic Mutual. The existence of CMG cyber-crime claim lines and mandatory training implies a steady claims flow.
- Abuse-survivor data held by bankruptcy professionals is a uniquely high-harm dataset, distinct from school data but relevant to "Catholic org data defense."

### Gaps
- No 2020-2026 documented ransomware incident at a US classical school was found.
- No 2020-2026 publicly reported parish BEC loss with a dollar figure was found in this pass. Searches returned only the 2019 St. Ambrose case.
- No 2026 Catholic incidents were found (searches not exhaustive).

## 5. Networks and associations that could distribute guidance at scale; existing initiatives

### Takeaway
The strongest existing channel is insurance. Catholic Mutual Group (the church's self-insurance mutual, covering most US dioceses) already mandates training through CMGConnect, ties coverage to MFA and EDR, and runs a cyber-claims line. Dioceses pass these requirements to parishes and schools. NCEA has no visible cybersecurity program, which is a gap. Australia's CEnet (a Catholic education network with a sector-wide CyberCX incident-response partnership) is a model with no US equivalent found. Classical networks are fragmented across ACCS, SCL, ICLE, Hillsdale, Great Hearts, and Opus Dei-affiliated independent schools.

### Cited Findings
- Catholic Mutual Group: CMGConnect mandatory cyber training (Milwaukee mandate 2022); claims reporting line 1-800-228-6108 x2444 / reportaclaim@catholicmutual.org; Catholic Mutual "CARES" cyber security practices handouts. — [archmil.org mandate](https://www.archmil.org/ArchMil/Resources/FIN/MandateCyberSecurity2022.09.06.pdf); [Des Moines claims reporting](https://www.dmdiocese.org/filesimages/Safety/Cyber%20Safety/Cyber%20Crime%20Claims%20Reporting.pdf); [Catholic Hawaii CARES](https://www.catholichawaii.org/media/649006/cyber-security-practices.pdf); [Catholic Hawaii tips](https://www.catholichawaii.org/media/649005/cyber-security-tips.pdf)

### Inferences
- Highest-leverage distribution order: (1) Catholic Mutual Group and diocesan risk managers, because guidance attached to insurance compliance gets implemented; (2) diocesan superintendents and Office of Catholic Schools tech leads, since they choose the SIS and run principal meetings; (3) the Catholic press syndicate (OSV News), which reaches every diocesan paper; (4) ICLE and Great Hearts for the classical side.
- A free "CMG-aligned parish/school security checklist" (MFA on M365/Google/FACTS/ParishSOFT, out-of-band verification for any bank-detail change, EDR, backups) could slot into existing mandates without competing with them.
