## 1. Checking eligibility without collecting documents (IVES, FA-DDX, state matches, VCs, ZK, Merit). Is it realistic for small SGOs in 2027?

### Takeaway
The strongest "verify, don't collect" pattern already running in U.S. education is **categorical eligibility by a data match with an agency**. School-meal direct certification against SNAP and Medicaid is the model, and the proposed 25F rules already allow categorical eligibility (SNAP, TANF, WIC, Section 8, SSI). **IRS IVES** is a working route to a transcript with consent, but it is built for lenders. Enrollment requires an IRS suitability check, and the SGO still receives a transcript, not a yes/no. **FA-DDX** shows that Congress can create a consent-based IRS-to-agency pipe in which applicants cannot edit the data. It is closed to SGOs. **SD-JWT (RFC 9901, Nov 2025)** and mDL selective disclosure are finished standards. **Zero-knowledge proofs** are deployed for age, not income: Google open-sourced Longfellow ZK in July 2025. No government ZK income-eligibility deployment was found. For a small SGO in 2027 the realistic options, in order: categorical eligibility through a state agency or a shared intermediary; an IVES transcript through an intermediary that keeps only the decision; documents as a last resort, deleted on a fixed schedule.

### Cited Findings
**25F rules (context from earlier notes)**
- The proposed and temporary 25F regulations (Oct 2026) let SGOs verify income by direct documentation (pay stubs, tax returns, W-2s, IRS transcripts) or by categorical eligibility (SNAP, TANF, WIC, Section 8, SSI within 12 months). Foster children are deemed eligible. Comments are due Dec 1, 2026, and the hearing is Dec 15, 2026. — [Brownstein Q&A](https://www.bhfs.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Federal-Scholarship-Tax-Credit-QA-102026_VFINAL.pdf); [Federal Register 2026-20277](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/02/2026-20277/federal-scholarship-tax-credit)

**IRS IVES (DEPLOYED, built for lenders)**
- IVES lets approved third parties get a taxpayer's transcripts with the taxpayer's consent. Consent is given through the taxpayer's IRS online account or on Form 4506-C (only the 10-22 revision is accepted). — [IRS IVES](https://irs.gov/individuals/income-verification-express-service); [IVES FAQs](https://www.irs.gov/node/10172)
- To enroll, a participant files Form 13803 and passes an IRS background and suitability check on the company and the individuals named. Requests go by fax (about 2 to 3 business days, to the Austin, Kansas City or Ogden campuses), by Application-to-Application (A2A), or through the WebUI portal. — [IVES enrollment](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/ives-enrollment-procedures); [IVES faxing](https://www.irs.gov/node/124751)
- Participants that accept e-signatures need separate approval and must keep audit logs and the signed forms for 2 years. The processing procedures in IRM 3.5.20 are effective 01-01-2026. — [IVES e-signature](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/how-to-get-started-using-ives-electronic-signature); [IRM 3.5.20](https://www.irs.gov/irm/part3/irm_03-005-020r)

**FAFSA FA-DDX (DEPLOYED; closed to SGOs)**
- The FUTURE Act requires ED to get applicants' (and parents' or spouses') tax information directly from the IRS through the FUTURE Act Direct Data Exchange (FA-DDX). FA-DDX replaced the IRS Data Retrieval Tool for 2024-25. Every contributor must consent each year, and non-filers must consent too. Contributors cannot change data that arrived through FA-DDX. — [UWF](https://uwf.edu/offices/financial-aid/applying-for-aid/federal-tax-info-and-data-sharing/); [NASFAA AskRegs](https://askregs.nasfaa.org/article/36817/will-the-fafsa-contributor-be-allowed-to-change-federal-tax-information)
- ED's guidance (GEN-25-08, Sept 30, 2025) defines FTI as all data received through FA-DDX plus anything derived from it that could be used to work back to FTI. Tax data that families type in by hand is "FAFSA data" instead. This "derived data is still protected" rule is a useful model for SGO regulation. — [FSA Partners GEN-25-08](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/dear-colleague-letters/2025-09-30/guidance-use-federal-tax-information-fti-free-application-federal-student-aid-fafsar-data-and-non-fafsa-data); ED-IRS matching agreement: [ED CMA](https://ed.gov/about/offices/list/om/docs/pirms/future-cma.pdf)

**Categorical eligibility by agency data match (DEPLOYED at national scale in school meals)**
- In the National School Lunch Program, states run direct certification by matching student records against SNAP (and in many states Medicaid) data and report match rates to USDA. USDA has prioritized states whose SNAP direct-certification rate is below 95%. — [Massachusetts DESE](https://doe.mass.edu/news/news.aspx?id=27484); [Colorado state document](https://spl.cde.state.co.us/artemis/edserials/ed518010internet/ed5180102024internet.pdf)
- 43 states used Medicaid data for direct certification (FRAC figure from 2024, may be out of date). USDA approved 12 new states for Direct Certification with Medicaid demonstrations in SY2023-24. — [FRAC](https://frac.org/?p=20536); [USDA DC-M](https://www.fna.usda.gov/cn/direct-certification-medicaid-demonstration-project)
- Weakness: Georgia's average direct-certification rate fell 6 points from 2023-24 to 2024-25, mainly because of Medicaid redeterminations. Match-based eligibility drifts with changes upstream. — [Georgia GOSA memo](https://gosa.georgia.gov/document/document/2025-direct-certification-memo/download); Johns Hopkins found Medicaid direct certification raised meal participation in Maryland — [JHU 2026](https://publichealth.jhu.edu/2026/study-medicaid-direct-certification-increased-school-meal-participation-in-maryland-schools)

**Verifiable credentials and selective disclosure (STANDARD)**
- SD-JWT is published as **RFC 9901** (Proposed Standard, Nov 2025; Fett, Yasuda, Campbell). It is a JWS plus optional Disclosures, so a holder can reveal only some claims. "SD-JWT+KB" adds key-binding proof of possession. — [RFC 9901](https://rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9901.html)
- Google open-sourced its ZKP library **Longfellow ZK** in July 2025 (https://github.com/google/longfellow-zk) for age assurance ("prove over 18 without sharing anything else"). It builds on a partnership with Sparkasse and is linked to the EU eIDAS digital-identity wallet. — [Google blog](https://blog.google/technology/safety-security/opening-up-zero-knowledge-proof-technology-to-promote-privacy-in-age-assurance/?hl=de). A Japanese trade article says the library underlies Google's Credential Manager API for wallet age requests — [ITmedia, Jul 2025](https://atmarkit.itmedia.co.jp/ait/articles/2507/28/news025.html)

**ZK income proofs (PILOT/PROPOSAL at most)**
- No confirmed government deployment of a ZK income-eligibility proof was found. The closest is a Cardano Catalyst funding proposal for "zero-knowledge tenant credentials": DID/VC with Pedersen-commitment range proofs on income and age, with an Irish housing authority named as the intended pilot host. Status is unverified. — [Project Catalyst](https://projectcatalyst.io/funds/14/cardano-use-cases-concepts/zero-knowledge-tenant-credentials-using-veridian-didvc). Vendor pages claim a 15-25% cut in improper payments, which is unsupported marketing — [Chainscore](https://www.chainscorelabs.com/use-cases/government-identity-and-public-services/public-benefit-distribution/zero-knowledge-eligibility-verification)

**Merit International (DEPLOYED as an ESA administrator)**
- Merit administered Ohio's Afterschool Child Enrichment (ACE) ESA under contract with the Ohio Department of Education. The program was in its second year in 2023. — [BusinessWire 2023](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230411005014/en/Ohio-Afterschool-Child-Enrichment-ACE-Expands-as-Program-Enters-Second-Year-Administered-by-Merit)
- Merit markets "real-time verification for licenses, credentials, and eligibility status" ([BusinessWire 2024](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20240627510200/en)). It bid on Iowa's Students First ESA administration in 2023 ([Iowa bid doc](https://bidopportunities.iowa.gov/Home/GetBidOpportunityDocument/457acf86-7ec1-4b70-8e6e-f7dc9eb50978)). Whether it won was not confirmed.

### Inferences
- **Design A, "eligibility oracle" (most practical for 2027).** A state, or a shared nonprofit intermediary serving all SGOs in a state, runs one IVES enrollment and/or a SNAP/Medicaid match. It returns a signed `{household_ref, program_year, eligible: true/false, basis: "categorical"|"income", income_band: "<=200%"|"<=300%", expires}`, issued as an SD-JWT. The SGO stores only the signed assertion. The intermediary discards the transcript after it decides (keep a hash plus the decision for audit). This puts IVES suitability vetting, IRS-grade security and FTI handling in one place instead of thousands of small SGOs. It is the FA-DDX pattern rebuilt from parts that already exist.
- **Design B, reusable credential.** The same assertion goes into the family's wallet so it can be shown to several SGOs. That cuts duplicate collection when a family applies to many SGOs, which is likely because donors spread credits around. Use SD-JWT VC now. ZK range proofs only become worth it once a wallet ecosystem exists, and none exists for U.S. income in 2027.
- **Why "income band" and not income.** 25F eligibility is tied to area-median-income multiples, as earlier notes describe the rules. Revealing only the band meets the SGO's needs. The precise AGI is the toxic asset.
- **Comment ask for Dec 1:** (a) say categorical eligibility and source-verified methods are preferred; (b) borrow GEN-25-08's "derived data that can be used to work back to FTI is still protected" rule for SGO-held tax documents; (c) require deletion of underlying documents after the decision is recorded; (d) allow, and let states fund, shared verification intermediaries.
- Realism: a two-person SGO enrolling in IVES itself is implausible (suitability checks, fax/A2A workflows, 2-year logging). Intermediaries (state treasurer, ESA vendor, statewide SGO association) are the real path. A ZK-based system is not realistic for 2027.

### Gaps
- Whether the IRS would admit SGOs or their intermediaries to IVES, and whether IVES can return only a yes/no, was not confirmed. IVES today returns transcripts.
- No source on state SNAP/Medicaid agencies' legal authority to answer SGO eligibility queries. SNAP data-sharing limits (7 U.S.C. 2020(e)(8)) are training knowledge, not checked.
- Merit's architecture (VC standards used, data kept) and current contracts were not confirmed.
- No direct page fetch of the 25F proposed rule text to check for recordkeeping or security language.

## 2. Minimization and retention automation in common tools (Google, Microsoft 365, form tools, financial-aid platforms)

### Takeaway
Auto-deletion exists in the tools small orgs already pay for, but often only on higher tiers. **Google Vault** retention rules can purge Drive and Gmail content. **Label-scoped** retention (for example, "Counseling: delete 1 year after graduation") needs Business Plus, Enterprise or Education Standard/Plus. **Jotform** has account-wide and per-form Auto-Delete Submissions (marketed as an Enterprise feature). **M365 Business Premium** includes sensitivity labels and encryption at nonprofit pricing of $5.50 per user per month. Automated retention labeling at scale sits in a $10 per user per month Purview add-on. Formstack's and financial-aid platforms' retention controls were not established.

### Cited Findings
- Google Vault retention rules can retain or purge Drive files. When a rule's coverage ends, files can take up to 15 days to be removed from Drive. Admins can recover them for 25 days after they leave trash. Rule changes take up to 24 hours to spread. **Holds override retention**, so an open legal hold blocks purges. Shared-drive files can only be purged by certain rule scopes. — [Retain Drive files with Vault](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/vault/retention/retain-drive-files-with-vault); [How retention works](https://support.google.com/vault/answer/2990828)
- Vault retention rules based on Drive labels (June 2023) are available to Business Plus, Enterprise Essentials/Essentials Plus/Standard/Plus, and Education Standard/Plus. **Business Standard is not listed.** — [Workspace Updates, Jun 2023](https://workspaceupdates.googleblog.com/2023/06/enhancing-google-vault-file-retention-capabilities-using-google-drive-labels.html)
- Gmail retention via Vault: [Retain Gmail messages with Vault](https://support.google.com/vault/answer/2535539?hl=en)
- Jotform Auto-Delete Submissions can be set account-wide (Account Settings > Data) or per form. Deleted items sit in Trash and can be restored for 30 days. **Submissions imported into Jotform Tables or added by hand are not auto-deleted.** Org-wide management is documented. — [Jotform help](https://www.jotform.com/help/how-to-delete-form-submissions-automatically/); [Org-wide auto delete](https://www.jotform.com/help/how-to-manage-org-wide-auto-delete-submissions/); [Enterprise blog](https://www.jotform.com/blog/auto-delete-submissions-feature-for-enterprise/). Older Jotform answers saying "no auto-deletion" are outdated — [Jotform answer](https://jotform.com/answers/19115491-will-jotform-ever-automatically-delete-or-clear-our-files-submissions-after-a-certain-amount-of-time)
- A Zapier template (receive a Jotform submission, wait, delete it) is a workaround for plans without native auto-delete. — [Zapier template](https://zapier.com/templates/details/584444803993f98e0021c6ea5f8c1b99bdf66110b75d8b540f81906a0b448f7f)
- M365 Business Premium includes sensitivity labels, DLP and encryption (Purview Message Encryption). — [Microsoft Learn: information protection in Business Premium](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/admin/security-and-compliance/m365bp-information-protection). Nonprofit staff pricing is $5.50 per user per month (annual) — [Microsoft nonprofit plans](https://www.microsoft.com/microsoft-365/business/nonprofit-plans-and-pricing). The Purview Suite add-on for Business Premium ($10 per user per month) adds automated classification, retention labeling and records management — [Microsoft Purview Suite for BP](https://microsoft.com/en-us/security/small-medium-business/microsoft-purview-suite-business-premium). A forum report says exactly which retention features the base plan includes is unclear — [Tech Community](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/discussions/microsoft-security/little-warning-on-the-new-purview-suite-for-m365bp/4453510/replies/4458815)
- Microsoft's education deployment guide covers data lifecycle management for schools — [Microsoft Learn: Education DLM](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/education/guide/4-advanced/security/advanced-security-data-lifecycle-management)

### Inferences
- **Pattern, "intake, then decide, then shred."** Every eligibility form gets a short TTL (for example 30 to 60 days after the decision). The decision record moves to a minimal table (applicant ref, decision, basis, date, reviewer). Attachments never land in personal Drive or email: the form tool writes to a single restricted folder covered by a purge rule.
- **Watch for "shadow copies."** Jotform exempts Tables. Email notifications carry attachments into Gmail. Zapier copies data to Sheets. Auto-delete on the form tool alone does not delete the data. Turn off attachments in notification emails, and put Gmail/Drive retention rules on the notification label.
- **Licensing trap.** Many small Catholic schools and nonprofits run Business Standard or Education Fundamentals. They can't use label-scoped purges, and Vault on Business Standard is limited. A low-cost alternative: one Google Group plus one shared drive dedicated to sensitive intake, with an org-unit-wide or shared-drive-scoped Vault rule.
- **Holds are the safety valve.** Since holds override purges, aggressive retention can coexist with litigation or child-protection holds. That answers the usual objection ("we might need it for a court").

### Gaps
- Formstack and Google Forms retention controls not checked. Google Forms has no native auto-delete as far as known (training knowledge). Responses in Sheets would be under Drive retention.
- Financial-aid platforms (FACTS Grant & Aid, TADS, Clarity/SSS) retention of uploaded tax returns not found.
- Exact Vault availability on Business Standard and Education Fundamentals not confirmed from a current edition matrix.

## 3. Privacy-preserving public reporting (small-cell suppression, DP, synthetic data; ESA/voucher reporting)

### Takeaway
Missouri's leak was not a statistics failure. Row-level records were posted on purpose and called "directory information." So the first control is architectural: **publish only aggregates produced by a fixed pipeline. Never publish an extract.** For the aggregates, the long-standing education practice is PTAC/NCES suppression: minimum cell sizes of 3 (absolute minimum) to 5 or 10, plus complementary suppression and top/bottom coding of extreme percentages. Differential privacy works but distorts small populations noticeably. DP synthetic data for small education datasets did poorly in a 2026 study. No ESA/voucher-specific federal reporting guidance was found.

### Cited Findings
- Missouri: the state treasurer's website posted MOScholars spreadsheets with three years of student names, parent contacts, award amounts and schools for nearly a year. The office called it "directory information" and blamed a contractor. — [Missouri Independent, Apr 2026](https://missouriindependent.com/2026/04/21/missouri-treasurers-office-posted-moscholars-student-data-on-its-website-for-nearly-a-year/); [Missouri Independent, Jul 2026](https://missouriindependent.com/2026/07/27/after-voucher-data-leak-missouri-treasurers-office-pointed-at-its-contractor/)
- PTAC disclosure-avoidance FAQ (2012, updated 2013): many statisticians treat a cell size of 3 as the absolute minimum, and larger minimums (5 or 10) lower risk further. — [DaSy/UNC copy of PTAC FAQ](https://fpg.unc.edu/sites/fpg.unc.edu/files/resource-files/DaSyDisclosureAvoidanceFAQ.pdf); PTAC webinar defines suppression and warns that **large cells near 0% or 100% are also disclosive** — [PTAC webinar](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/sites/default/files/training/supporting_materials/Webinar_DD_Nov2012Final_0.pdf)
- Complementary suppression keeps a suppressed value from being worked out from totals. — [Connecticut SDE disclosure avoidance](https://portal.ct.gov/-/media/sde/performance/data-requests/ctdisclosureavoidance082014.pdf)
- Worked state policies: DC OSSE Student Privacy and Data Suppression Policy (2022) — [OSSE](https://osse.dc.gov/sites/default/files/dc/sites/osse/publication/attachments/OSSE%20Student%20Privacy%20and%20Data%20Suppression%20Policy_FINAL_1.27.22.pdf); Washington OSPI "Protecting Student Privacy in Public Reporting" (2023) — [OSPI](https://ospi.k12.wa.us/sites/default/files/2023-10/protecting-student-privacy-public-reporting_adav3_92023.pdf). Other sectors use larger thresholds: CMS suppresses cells of 1 to 10 — [HHS CMS policy](https://www.hhs.gov/guidance/document/cms-cell-suppression-policy)
- NCES SLDS Technical Brief 3 (NCES 2011-603, "Statistical Methods for Protecting PII in Aggregate Reporting") is the canonical NCES reference. Its text was not retrieved here.
- DP in education: a simulation across 67 Georgia districts found stronger privacy on student counts reduced how much alternative attendance boundaries could cut segregation — [arXiv 2305.07762](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.07762). Census-based analysis: DP and swapping both have larger error for small populations, but DP is more predictable — [arXiv 2308.12876](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.12876). DP framework for learning analytics (2025 preprint) — [arXiv 2501.01786](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.01786v1). DP synthetic data on small education datasets: on average only 36% of synthetic findings held up on real data (2026 preprint) — [arXiv 2604.01821](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.01821)

### Inferences
- **Design C, "transparency by query, not by file."** The program keeps microdata in a protected store. The public portal serves only pre-defined aggregate tables (by school, grade band, county, award-amount band), generated by code that enforces: n >= 10 per cell (a stricter choice than PTAC's 3/5 floor, justified by small private schools); complementary suppression; percentages coded as "<5%" or ">95%"; and award amounts rounded or banded. Ad-hoc requests go through the same engine. Any table that would identify a school with fewer than N scholarship students is merged into a "small schools in county X" group.
- **Linkage risk is real.** Voucher data combined with a private school's public enrollment and a parish bulletin can single out a family even from counts. Suppression thresholds should apply at the school level, not just the state level.
- **DP is optional, not required.** For annual counts by school, suppression plus rounding is enough and easy to explain. DP (for example the Laplace mechanism with a published epsilon) earns its place when the program publishes many overlapping cross-tabs or a query interface, where differencing attacks become feasible. Don't publish synthetic microdata. The 2026 evidence suggests it isn't good enough for analysis on small education datasets.
- **Pre-publication gate (cheap, deterministic).** Before any file goes on a public URL, run a CI-style check that rejects files with name/email/phone/address-like columns or any row-level student identifier, and that flags tables with cells below the threshold. This would have caught Missouri. It could be an open-source tool from the project (the "publish lint" idea).
- **Contract clause.** ESA/SGO administrator contracts should define "public report" as aggregates meeting a written suppression standard and state that FERPA "directory information" designations never apply to program participation data.

### Gaps
- No ED or state guidance written specifically for ESA/voucher/SGO public reporting was found. Need to check state statutes (e.g., AZ, FL, IA) for report contents.
- NCES 2011-603 exact recommendations not retrieved.
- Whether Missouri changed its publication process after the leak not confirmed.

## 4. Protecting the most sensitive child records (counseling, IEP, safeguarding): separation, client-side encryption, ASCA/social-work standards

### Takeaway
Professional standards already require separation. ASCA: keep sole-possession records apart from education records, keep them minimal, purge on a set schedule. FERPA's sole-possession exception covers only memory aids that are never shared, and electronic notes often don't qualify. Technically, **Google Workspace client-side encryption (CSE)** is the strongest option inside a tool small schools already use. It requires Enterprise Plus, Education Standard/Plus (Frontline Plus on one page), an external key service, and it breaks delegation and some mobile editing. For orgs on lower tiers, a separate end-to-end-encrypted workspace (Proton, Tresorit) for a small safeguarding team is the practical route.

### Cited Findings
- ASCA standards: "Keeps sole-possession records separate from students' educational records in keeping with state laws." Counselors should set a reasonable purge timeline (for example, shred at transition, transfer or graduation) and take care before destroying records a court may need. — [ASCA Ethical Standards (2016 text)](https://www.bcbe.org//cms/lib/AL01901374/Centricity/Domain/5415/ASCA%20Ethical%20Standards%20for%20School%20Counselors%202016.pdf); [ASCA current standards page](https://www.schoolcounselor.org/About-School-Counseling/Ethical-Responsibilities/ASCA-Ethical-Standards-for-School-Counselors-(1)); [2022 changes](https://schoolcounselor.org:443/getmedia/a2ef12e2-6b65-4df3-96f7-a8d376b52d12/2022-ethical-standards-changes.pdf)
- FERPA sole-possession records (34 CFR 99.3): memory aids not shared or accessible to others. Notes should be minimal (name, date, brief reminder). Electronic notes often fall under FERPA, and FPCO has never clearly defined where the line is. — [Montana State journal](https://jsc.montana.edu/articles/v16n18.pdf); [ASCA magazine 2023](https://schoolcounselor.org/Magazines/March-April-2023/Parental-Rights-and-Information-Sharing)
- Google Workspace CSE: supported editions are Enterprise Plus and Education Standard/Plus. One page also lists Frontline Plus, so pages disagree. The org must supply its own keys through a partner key service or the CSE API. Users on consumer Gmail accounts can't open CSE content. **Gmail CSE does not support delegation (shared inboxes).** Encrypted Docs/Sheets/Slides are not supported on Drive mobile. Hardware smart-card keys for Gmail require the Assured Controls add-on. — [CSE setup overview](https://knowledge.workspace.google.com/admin/security/client-side-encryption-setup-overview); [Google support 14311764](https://support.google.com/a/answer/14311764)
- M365 Business Premium sensitivity labels can apply encryption (service-side, Microsoft holds keys unless an add-on is used). — [Microsoft Learn BP information protection](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-365/admin/security-and-compliance/m365bp-information-protection)

### Inferences
- **Design D, "sensitive-records enclave."** (1) Sort records into tiers: Tier 0 (general), Tier 1 (IEP/504, medical), Tier 2 (counseling notes, safeguarding/abuse reports, custody orders). (2) Tier 2 lives only in an enclave: a CSE-labeled shared drive (if licensed), or an E2EE workspace (Proton Drive/Tresorit business) for named staff only, or locked paper for true sole-possession notes. (3) Email carries only pointers ("New note filed for student ref 4821"), never content. Enforce with DLP or a Gmail content-compliance rule that blocks or quarantines messages containing keywords plus student names to external recipients. (4) Vault/Purview retention on the enclave matches the purge timeline, with holds for court matters.
- **Cryptographic angle.** CSE and E2EE tools protect against the provider and stolen admin sessions, but not against a phished endpoint of an authorized user. Pair the enclave with phishing-resistant MFA (passkeys) for the few enclave users. That is a short list, so cheap to fund.
- **Separation reduces breach scope more than encryption does.** The biggest win is moving counseling and custody material out of Gmail threads and "Shared with me" sprawl. A quarterly Drive audit for files shared "anyone with the link" that contain sensitive keywords is free (Workspace admin investigation tool or Drive audit log; training knowledge).
- IEP records: in private schools, IEPs/ISPs are often produced by the public district (IDEA equitable services), so the private school may hold only copies and can minimize to the service plan summary.

### Gaps
- Proton and Tresorit business features and pricing for nonprofits not researched this session. Should be confirmed before recommending.
- NASW/social-work record standards and child-protection (safeguarding) record standards (e.g., VIRTUS in Catholic dioceses) not searched. See `child_safeguarding.md`.
- No source on the share of small schools licensed for Education Standard/Plus (needed for CSE).

## 5. Passing "protected child" status between apps (SIS flags, directory suppression, OneRoster/Ed-Fi, gaps; an open schema?)

### Takeaway
SISs store directory opt-outs as **local fields** (PowerSchool "Directory Information Opt Out (FERPA)" on the Other Info page; Infinite Campus FERPA record with a multi-value `dataOptOut`). Custody and restraining-order information is typically a **free-text alert set up by each district**, not a structured field from the vendor. **OneRoster 1.2 (Sept 2022) has no core privacy or protection flag.** Only regional profiles and vendor `metadata` extensions carry such data. No Ed-Fi standard element was found. So when roster data syncs to the LMS, photo app, yearbook, messaging and fundraising tools, the "do not publish / do not release to person X" status is lost. This is a real standards gap that a small open schema plus a checklist could fill.

### Cited Findings
- PowerSchool (district guidance): the opt-out is set on each student's "Other Info" page under "Directory Information Opt Out (FERPA)", with a stored search to list opted-out students. — [Springfield Public Schools KB](https://springfieldpublicschools.teamdynamix.com/TDClient/1908/Portal/KB/ArticleDet?ID=164072); NC DPI military-recruiter opt-out reference — [NC DPI](https://www.dpi.nc.gov/documents/student-info/ps-qrd-military-recruitment-v2/open)
- Infinite Campus: a FERPA record with a `dataOptOut` field ("Multiple values may be selected to indicate which data is not to be shared"). — [Infinite Campus KB export](https://kb.infinitecampus.com/help/pdfexport/id/66631cd148fc97cb4c0152d1)
- Court orders in PowerSchool (district practice): entered as Guardian Alerts under Custom Screens, with an expiration date (e.g., restraining order). ### Inferences
- **Design E, open "Protection Profile" schema (proposal).** A small JSON object carried in the OneRoster `metadata` (e.g., `metadata.ext.protection`) and as a CSV extension column:
  ```json
  {"v":1,
   "directory_optout": ["photo","name_public","address","phone","yearbook","media"],
   "do_not_release_to": [{"ref":"guardian-17","basis":"court_order","expires":"2027-06-30"}],
   "location_confidential": true,
   "alias_display_name": "J. S.",
   "contact_school_before_disclosure": true}
  ```
  It holds **no reasons, no case details, no names of restrained parties**, only references and behaviors. Receivers must apply the most restrictive reading (fail closed when the field is missing or unparseable for students flagged as protected).
- **Behavior contract for receivers (checklist):** suppress in public galleries, newsletters, yearbook and social posts; hide address and phone from staff directories and class lists; block pickup or release to listed refs; exclude from fundraising and marketing exports; bar from AI photo-tagging and face matching; log any access. Vendors self-attest per item, which is lightweight and similar to the Student Privacy Pledge.
- **Why it matters for address-confidentiality program (ACP) families.** State ACPs give a substitute address, but the real address leaks if the SIS address field syncs to a bus, lunch or fundraising vendor. `location_confidential: true` should mean "send the ACP substitute address or nothing."
- Practical first step without vendors: a quarterly "propagation test." Pick a test student with all flags set, then check every connected app (LMS, photo, messaging, yearbook, donor CRM) to see whether the flags survived. Small orgs can do this in an hour.

## 6. Children's identity protection after breaches (minor credit freezes, IP PIN for dependents, SSN minimization)

### Takeaway
Three cheap, durable post-breach measures: **(1) free child credit freezes** (federal law since Sept 21, 2018, for children under 16, requested by a parent; bureaus want proof of parent identity and authority plus the child's identity documents, which means more documents sent, so it's a trade-off); **(2) an IRS IP PIN for the dependent** (Form 15227 if AGI is below $84k single or $168k MFJ, or an in-person visit with the child's birth certificate and SS card). Since the 2025 filing season, the IRS accepts duplicate-dependent returns when an IP PIN is used. **(3) don't collect SSNs at all.** No single federal law governs K-12 SSN collection. California bans collecting SSNs, even the last four digits, unless another law requires it. Maine requires notice of purpose and an opt-out, and deletion of SSNs once the student leaves. A federal audit found schools often use SSNs out of convenience.

### Cited Findings
- Free credit freezes for minors under 16 since Sept 21, 2018 (S. 2155). — [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/about-us/blog/new-protections-available-minors-under-16); [FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/node/298282); state "protected consumer" law context — [CA AG](https://oag.ca.gov/idtheft/facts/freeze-child-credit)
- Parents and guardians can request IP PINs for dependents. Form 15227 applies if AGI is below $84,000 (individual) or $168,000 (MFJ) and the online account can't be set up. In-person visits need two IDs for the dependent (e.g., birth certificate, SS card). The PIN arrives by USPS in about 4 to 6 weeks. Don't refile Form 15227 for a lost PIN. — [IRS Get an IP PIN](https://www.irs.gov/ippin); [Taxpayer Advocate, Feb 2026](https://www.taxpayeradvocate.irs.gov/news/nta-blog/protect-yourself-from-tax-related-identity-theft-get-an-identity-protection-pin/2026/02)
- From the 2025 filing season the IRS accepts duplicate-dependent returns that include a valid IP PIN for the dependent. — [IRS newsroom](https://irs.gov/newsroom/irs-takes-steps-to-help-prevent-refund-delays-by-accepting-duplicate-dependent-returns-with-an-ip-pin-for-2025-filing-season-taxpayers-encouraged-to-sign-up-soon-for-ip-pin-online-account)
- California Ed Code 49076.7: districts, county offices and charter schools may not collect SSNs or their last four digits from pupils or parents unless required by law. — [Cal. Educ. Code 49076.7](https://california.public.law/codes/education_code_section_49076.7)
- Maine 20-A 6001-C: schools must state the purpose, allow an opt-out, and remove SSNs from records once the student is no longer enrolled. — [Maine statute](https://legislature.maine.gov/legis/statutes/20-A/title20-Asec6001-C.html); ACLU of Maine criticism — [ACLU](https://wp.api.aclu.org/press-releases/mclu-urges-school-boards-review-social-security-number-collection-program)
- SSA OIG audit: no single federal law regulates SSN use by K-12 schools; many schools used SSNs as identifiers when other identifiers would have worked. — [SSA OIG A-08-10-11057](https://oig-files.ssa.gov/audits/summary/html/A-08-10-11057.html)
- Florida schools collected SSNs without telling families they could decline (2017 reporting). — [WUFT](https://www.wuft.org/news/2017/05/19/find-out-florida-why-do-schools-still-collect-students-social-security-numbers/)

### Inferences
- **SSN minimization pattern.** Enrollment forms: remove the SSN field. Where state reporting truly needs it, collect it in a separate one-time, encrypted form that the SIS stores in a restricted field, and delete it at withdrawal (following Maine's rule). Financial aid: rely on SGO/aid-platform verification (section 1) instead of collecting dependents' SSNs from 1040s. A redaction instruction ("black out SSNs before upload") is a weak control. Accepting IRS transcripts, which mask SSNs, is stronger. (That transcripts mask SSNs is training knowledge, not checked this session.)
- **Post-breach playbook for small orgs.** Notify with a pre-written child-specific guide: freeze at all three bureaus, get the child's IP PIN, and check for an existing credit file (a child with a file is already a victim). Fund it through cyber insurance (see `economics_insurance_funding.md`). Note the trade-off: a freeze request sends a birth certificate and SS card copy to bureaus, which is itself a disclosure. It is worth it only when the SSN is actually exposed.
- **Preventive "pre-breach freeze" campaign.** Since freezes are free and permanent until lifted, schools could encourage families to freeze children's credit at enrollment, ahead of any breach. This cuts the harm of any future school breach at almost no cost.

### Gaps
- Current bureau-specific document requirements and online versus mail options for minor freezes not re-verified this session (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion pages not fetched).
- Whether federal programs (e.g., E-Rate, Title I, Medicaid school billing) require private schools to collect student SSNs was not checked. Medicaid school-based billing likely uses Medicaid IDs (training knowledge).
- A 50-state SSN-collection law survey was not done. California, Maine and Washington (1997-98 bill) are examples only.
