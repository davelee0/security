# Make the paperwork tell the truth

The most valuable thing davelee0/security can do next is help children's data protection catch up with what it already claims to be, rather than add more guides. Across all eight expert lenses the same failure keeps showing up: protections exist on paper (custody orders, insurance renewal questionnaires, vendor data privacy agreements, COPPA's new written-program duties, NYC's 2-d disclosures, diocesan Safe Environment rules) and nothing checks whether the systems actually honor them. Three things the project has not yet absorbed change what to build. First, **the money loss is mostly email and payment fraud, which accounts for about 58-60% of small-business cyber insurance claims** ([Coalition 2025](https://www.coalitioninc.com/blog/2025-cyber-claims-report); [Risk & Insurance](https://riskandinsurance.com/business-email-compromise-claims-jump-30-as-a-i-reshapes-cyber-risk/)). Second, **criminals now pressure parents and children directly**, and paying does not get data deleted ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/powerschool-paid-off-hackers-after-huge-breach-now-theyre-extorting-districts/); [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2025/09/hackers-threaten-parents-get-nursery-to-pay-ransom-or-we-leak-your-childs-data)). Third, **the most damaging data is child-protection content**: counseling notes, abuse reports, custody details and psychological evaluations ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/from-campus-rape-cases-to-child-abuse-reports-worst-case-data-breach-rocks-mn-schools/)). Weighing impact, novelty and feasibility for one volunteer working with Claude, five directions come out on top: (1) a "protected child" kit that makes a custody order or confidential-address status carry through every app, roster and photo gallery; (2) a vendor letter asking for the written security program and retention policy that the amended COPPA Rule has required since April 22, 2026, with Claude reading the answers; (3) a Safe Environment data-hygiene module for parishes; (4) a "make your insurance attestation true" kit aimed at diocesan risk managers; and (5) a payment-verification protocol built to survive voice cloning. All five use public information only, test nobody's systems, and build directly on the domain checkup, the Google Workspace guide and the NYC 2-d study.

## The threat model is wrong in four ways that matter

The project's DIRECTION.md already puts impersonation and payment fraud first. The new evidence makes that ranking stronger and adds a detail that changes which controls to recommend: **ordinary MFA no longer stops the fraud by itself**. In a Massachusetts district payroll diversion in 2025, attackers **intercepted MFA** in real time, took over payroll and email accounts, and the loss surfaced months later ([Sudbury Weekly](https://sudburyweekly.com/sps-hit-by-payroll-cyberattack-in-2025-over-12000-diverted/)). Ohio auditors found districts had no verification policy for direct-deposit changes until after they lost money ([Ohio Auditor](https://ohioauditor.gov/news/PressReleases/Details/7799)). Vendor-impersonation losses run from $1.3M to $4.92M per incident ([Valley News Live](https://www.valleynewslive.com/2026/02/11/north-dakota-school-district-loses-nearly-5-million-sophisticated-email-scam/?outputType=amp); [ClickOrlando](https://clickorlando.com/news/local/2024/03/04/13m-lost-after-scammers-target-seminole-county-public-schools)), far more than a small school would plausibly pay in ransom. The canonical parish case, a $1.75M construction-payment diversion through two compromised staff mailboxes, ended with the parish going back to paper checks ([SC Media](https://www.scmagazine.com/news/st-ambrose-catholic-parish-in-brunswick-ohio-was-hit-with-a-business-email-compromise-scam)). The control that actually stops the loss is a process, not a product: a hold on bank-detail changes, a callback to a number already on file, and a second approver.

AI is now aimed at that callback. Fully automated AI spear-phishing got a **54% click rate, the same as human experts and 4.5 times the 12% for generic phishing** ([Heiding et al., arXiv 2412.00586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.00586)). The FBI's first dedicated AI section counted **22,364 complaints and $893M in losses for 2025**, which it calls an undercount ([SecurityWeek](https://securityweek.com/fbi-cybercrime-losses-neared-21-billion-in-2025)). Coalition attributes a roughly 30% rise in email-fraud claims to AI phishing kits aimed at smaller firms ([Risk & Insurance](https://riskandinsurance.com/business-email-compromise-claims-jump-30-as-a-i-reshapes-cyber-risk/)). The evidence also has a limit: no source documents a US school or parish losing a wire transfer to a cloned voice. The documented school voice-clone cases are reputational, such as a fabricated recording of a Maryland principal ([CBS Baltimore](https://www.cbsnews.com/baltimore/news/maryland-pikesville-principal-ai-voice-impersonation-trial)), or extortion of families through fake "virtual kidnapping" calls using a child's voice ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/10/24/ai-voice-cloning-scam-fake-kidnapping-safe-word/)). The defensible claim is that a callback alone becomes weak once a voice can be faked, so the protocol needs a pre-shared code word or a two-person rule. "Listen carefully" is not a control.

The second shift is that **extortion has moved from the institution to the family**. Districts have repeatedly understated leaks. Medusa's Minneapolis dump included about 2,000 student psychological assessments and child-abuse inquiries ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/from-campus-rape-cases-to-child-abuse-reports-worst-case-data-breach-rocks-mn-schools/)). A Rhode Island district said no student data was exposed while sexual-misconduct complaints and special-education files were being posted ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/providence-students-sensitive-data-exposed-in-cyberattack-district-denies-leak/)). After PowerSchool paid about $2.9M for deletion, individual districts were extorted again months later ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/powerschool-paid-off-hackers-after-huge-breach-now-theyre-extorting-districts/)). The UK Kido nursery attackers posted profiles of ten children and **phoned parents** ([Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/news/2025/09/hackers-threaten-parents-get-nursery-to-pay-ransom-or-we-leak-your-childs-data)). Small organizations therefore need a pre-written family letter before anything happens. It should tell parents not to engage, not to pay, and to report, and it should walk them through the **free credit freeze for children under 16 that has existed in every state since September 2018**, which protects a child far better than adult-style credit monitoring of a file that does not exist ([FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/consumer-alerts/2019/03/new-protections-available-minors-under-16)).

The third shift reframes what "sensitive data" means. Identity theft is real (Javelin puts it at 1 in 43 children compromised in a single year, per [Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/little-understood-child-id-theft-reaching-crisis/)), but the safeguarding harm comes from **child-protection content**: counseling notes, IEP and 504 files, abuse reports and custody arrangements. Small organizations usually keep these in email and shared drives, outside any system with access controls. A New York City school's 2025 malware incident copied social-emotional assessment scores and social-worker meeting records from local computers ([NYC DOE incidents page](https://www.schools.nyc.gov/about-us/policies/data-privacy-and-security-policies/data-security-incidents)). The project's planned Workspace posture check should therefore look for where these records sit, not only for MFA gaps.

The fourth shift is the hardest to see from a security seat. **Legal protections for a targeted child only work if every system honors them.** FERPA gives both parents equal record access by default, and an abusive non-custodial parent can use that access to find a child or the other parent ([MassLegalHelp](https://masslegalhelp.org/es/node/1317)). New York's Address Confidentiality Program explicitly counts a **school address** as protected ([NY DOS statute](https://dos.ny.gov/nys-acp-statute)). Pickup, bus-tracking and admissions apps have nonetheless leaked exactly the "where is this child at 3pm" data an abuser wants. Examples include a Quebec school and daycare pickup app the province pulled ([CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/montreal/article/quebec-school-app-pulled-offline-after-major-security-flaw-exposes-parents-data/)), a K-12 bus portal that exposed student locations ([Tenable](https://www.tenable.com/blog/the-kids-arent-alright-vulnerabilities-in-edulog-portal-revealed-k-12-student-location-data)), and a 2026 admissions-site bug that exposed children's addresses and photos ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/19/bug-in-student-admissions-website-exposed-childrens-personal-information)). In one documented case, a father bought **Facebook ads geofenced to a half-mile around his children's school** asking strangers for photos of them ([The Columbian](https://columbian.com/news/2024/jul/14/i-want-her-to-worry-about-whos-waiting-on-the-corner-how-one-man-uses-facebook-to-frighten-his-childrens-mother-and-why-police-do-nothing)). Only **39% of schools studied post a FERPA directory opt-out form online** ([World Privacy Forum](https://worldprivacyforum.org/?p=7233)). Most private preschools, parish religious-education programs and youth leagues have no opt-out process at all. In the safeguarding research, no organization was found that helps a small school or parish make its records and apps follow a court order. NCMEC, Thorn, NNEDV and Cornell Tech's Clinic to End Tech Abuse (CETA) help individuals. VIRTUS, CMG Connect and Praesidium handle compliance. The work of connecting the two is unclaimed.

## Attestation without verification runs through every layer

The NYC 2-d study found that 95.7% of vendors pick the identical encryption sentence, and that Illuminate's disclosure is still posted unchanged years after regulators found it false. That is not a quirk of New York. The research found the same pattern, a claim made with nobody checking it, in six other places, and each one offers a verification point that a volunteer can reach with public information.

| Layer | What gets attested | Evidence nobody checks | Verification point a volunteer can reach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vendor contracts | DPA promises on data collection and sharing | Utah tested 85 DPA-covered apps: **52% collected data their agreement did not permit; 13% shared unpermitted data with third parties** ([GovTech](https://www.govtech.com/education/k-12/what-happened-when-utah-put-ed-tech-privacy-promises-to-the-test)) | SDPC registry of 130,000+ signed DPAs ([WSIPC](https://wsipc.org/news/version-two-of-sdpcs-national-data-privacy-agreement-is-available)) joined with ISL observed-traffic data ([ISOC Foundation](https://www.isocfoundation.org/project/us-k12-edtech-safety-benchmark-findings-data-publication/)) |
| Federal vendor duties | Amended COPPA Rule: written security program, written retention policy, separate consent before training AI on children's data, compliance due **April 22, 2026** ([Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule); [Data Protection Report](https://www.dataprotectionreport.com/2025/06/ftcs-coppa-rule-changes-include-ai-training-consent-requirement/)) | Schools rarely ask to see the documents | A one-page request letter, with the answers read by an LLM |
| Insurance | Renewal questionnaires attest to MFA, EDR and backups | One diocese's own business-office deck notes 27 locations lacked the EDR its insurer required ([Des Moines deck](https://www.dmdiocese.org/filesimages/Finance/Business%20Office%20Forums/Cyber%20Liability%20PPT.pdf)); academic work finds insurers collect limited security data at underwriting ([Woods et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.04713)) | Diocesan risk managers, renewal deadlines |
| Public incident reporting | NYC DOE lists 7 "incidents of note" | The State Comptroller counted **141 incidents from January 2023 to February 2025** ([OSC audit](https://www.osc.ny.gov/state-agencies/audits/2026/04/23/privacy-and-security-student-data)) | FOIL for the incident log |
| Oversight capacity | Common Sense ran edtech privacy reviews of 3,000+ tools | Its site says **"edtech reviews are currently paused"** ([Common Sense Privacy](https://privacy.commonsense.org/resource/tier-risks)) | LLM policy analysis is accurate enough for triage: average **F1 0.94 across 34 clauses** ([Xie et al., USENIX Security 2025](https://faculty.cc.gatech.edu/~frankli/papers/qinge_usenix2025.pdf)) |
| Safe Environment | Volunteers "cleared" in VIRTUS or CMG Connect | Parish site admins can see background-check results ([St. Cloud guide](https://stcdio.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/CMG-Site-Admin-User-Guide-2024.pdf)); no public retention or security policy found for VIRTUS, CMG Connect or Praesidium, while MinistrySafe publishes one ([MinistrySafe](https://ministrysafe.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/DATA-RETENTION-POLICY.pdf)) | Coordinator offboarding and MFA; asking vendors for a retention policy |

The legal lens explains why verification has to go through vendors and money rather than regulation of small organizations. ED's own FAQ says private and parochial K-12 schools "generally" fall outside FERPA ([studentprivacy.ed.gov](https://studentprivacy.ed.gov/faq/which-educational-agencies-or-institutions-does-ferpa-apply)). Most state comprehensive privacy laws exempt nonprofits, or set volume thresholds no parish school will reach ([Wiley](https://wiley.law/alert-New-State-Privacy-Laws-May-Apply-to-Nonprofit-Organizations)). NY Ed Law 2-d covers "educational agencies" ([NYSED definitions](https://www.nysed.gov/data-privacy-security/education-law-section-2-d-definitions)). For a typical Catholic, classical or nonprofit childcare program, the binding duties are state breach-notification law, licensing rules and contracts. Its **vendors**, however, are bound by COPPA and SOPIPA-style operator laws. The FTC's Illuminate order sets out what "reasonable" looks like: MFA, a data inventory, a public retention schedule, honesty about breach-notification timing, and biennial assessments ([Covington](https://www.insideprivacy.com/united-states/federal-trade-commission/ftc-announces-10-year-information-security-consent-orders-with-illuminate-education-and-illusory-systems/)). When EPIC commented on the proposed order, the final version gained an explicit data-minimization clause ([EPIC](https://epic.org/ftc-finalizes-settlement-with-illuminate-education-heeding-epics-call-to-strengthen-data-minimization-requirements/)). That is proof that a public comment on a consent order can change binding terms.

The economics lens explains why guides alone have not moved behavior. MFA is free in Microsoft 365 Business Basic and Google Workspace, and CISA's vulnerability scanning is free. Yet only about 1,500 schools had enrolled in CISA scanning in an older count ([K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/cisa-k12-cybersecurity-resources-frazier/720436/)), and email fraud still dominates claims. Harm is delayed and lands on families. Class actions go after deep-pocketed vendors like PowerSchool, which faces more than 30 federal suits ([The 74](https://www.the74million.org/article/wisconsin-district-sues-ed-tech-giant-powerschool-after-massive-data-breach/)), not a parish school. The levers that work are checkable conditions tied to money or permission to operate. A 2025 study of small firms found insurance prompts baseline controls but only up to the minimum required ([Computers & Security](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167404825005073)). For Catholic schools, that money condition already exists. Catholic Mutual insures Church-owned institutions in more than 125 dioceses and has run cyber training mandates through them ([Archdiocese of Milwaukee](https://www.archmil.org/ArchMil/Resources/FIN/MandateCyberSecurity2022.09.06.pdf)). The Archdiocese of Seattle now requires every parish, school and ministry to adopt its "Guardian" baseline ([Seattle mandate](https://archseattle.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Guardian-Mandate-Final.pdf)). Whether Catholic Mutual verifies anything it asks policyholders to attest to remains the single most important unknown.

Two quieter losses compound this. Microsoft ended free Business Premium for nonprofits effective each organization's first renewal after July 1, 2025. Business Premium is the tier that includes Conditional Access and Defender, and the transition is not automatic ([Withum](https://www.withum.com/resources/microsoft-nonprofit-pricing-changes-grant-donation-365-business-premium-e1/)), so many small parishes have probably dropped to Business Basic without noticing. MS-ISAC went fee-based on October 1, 2025, and K-12 members lost free malicious-domain blocking ([CIS FAQ](https://www.cisecurity.org/ms-isac/ms-isac-membership-faq); [Armorpoint](https://armorpoint.com/2025/10/24/what-the-ms-isac-transition-means-for-k-12-and-public-education/)). Each loss can be checked and fixed in about 15 minutes, by the organization itself.

### 1. Make a court order follow the child through every system

Build a two-page "protected child" workflow for when a custody order, ACP enrollment, foster placement or McKinney-Vento status reaches the front office. It lists every place the child's name, photo, schedule or location lives: SIS, childcare app, pickup app, bus app, yearbook, class photo galleries, parish directory, Flocknote or Remind lists, and teachers' social accounts. For each one it gives the exact steps to suppress the child, plus a front-office script for callers asking "is X in Ms. Y's class?" Every input already exists. Virginia's model policy has principals record an order's receipt and expiration in the student record ([Virginia DOE](https://doe.virginia.gov/home/showpublisheddocument/37255/638060299668570000)). Federal homelessness guidance says a McKinney-Vento student's address cannot be released even where directory information could be ([NAEHCY](https://naehcy.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Privacy-and-Homelessness-FERPA-2016.pdf)). Texas guidance names staff contacted by an alleged abuser as a recognized risk ([TEA](https://tea.texas.gov/system/files/fcss_rg-ch06.pdf)). What is missing is the translation into app settings, which is the project's core skill. It fits DIRECTION.md's "protecting a child's records is part of protecting the child" frame exactly. **Next steps:** draft the checklist for the five most common platforms in the target sector (Google Workspace, FACTS, brightwheel or Procare, Flocknote, a pickup app). Ask CETA at Cornell Tech in NYC, which already works on survivors' location-sharing problems ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/privacy-professionals-need-to-be-aware-of-tech-abuse)), and Advocates for Children of New York, which publishes guidance on students in DV shelters ([AFC NY](https://advocatesforchildren.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/supporting_students_in_dv_shelters.pdf)), to review it. Test it with one pilot school's front office.

### 2. Ask vendors for the documents the law now makes them keep

Since April 22, 2026, every for-profit online service collecting data from children under 13 has had to maintain a written information security program and a written retention policy with no indefinite retention. Disclosing children's data to train AI needs separate verifiable parental consent ([Loeb & Loeb](https://www.loeb.com/en/insights/publications/2025/05/childrens-online-privacy-in-2025-the-amended-coppa-rule); [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/new-coppa-obligations-for-ai-technologies-collecting-data-from-children)). This turns an attestation into an artifact that a small school can request. Under the FTC's school-authorization practice, a school consenting on parents' behalf has a legitimate reason to ask for it ([Wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-FTC-Adopts-Amended-Childrens-Online-Protection-Act-Rule)). The build is a one-page letter: "please send your COPPA written security program summary, your retention schedule, your subprocessor list, your breach-notification commitment to us in days, and confirm whether you use our students' data to train AI." It comes with a Claude-assisted reader that quotes the exact clause behind each finding and says "unclear" rather than guessing. Benchmark the answers against the Illuminate order terms. This is the 2-d study's "ask questions that can be false" made portable to private schools and childcare centers, which no state law reaches. **Next steps:** write the letter and a 10-rule lint list (AI training, indefinite retention, ad sharing, no subprocessor list, no breach timeline, missing MFA for admins and parents). Test the reader on 20 public privacy policies from childcare and parish vendors and hand-check every finding before claiming accuracy. Contract text has not been benchmarked the way privacy policies have. Publish only the template and the rules, never vendor-specific scores for small vendors.

### 3. Secure the Safe Environment data every parish already holds

Each year Safe Environment programs train **2.3 million adults and 2.8 million children** and process background checks that include volunteers' SSNs ([USCCB 2025 CYP report](https://www.usccb.org/resources/2025%20CYP%20Annual%20Report%20Digital%20Version.pdf); [Arlington OPCYP](https://opcypsupport.arlingtondiocese.org/support/solutions/articles/44002152724-opcyp-pii-security)). Part-time parish coordinators hold logins to portals that expose that data. Two further risks are almost entirely unaddressed. The first is abuse-allegation and victim files. In 2025 a bankruptcy consultant (BRG) and creditors' counsel (Stinson) working on diocesan Chapter 11 cases were breached, exposing abuse-survivor data ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/bankruptcy-law/bankrupt-catholic-groups-data-stolen-in-brg-ransomware-attack); [BishopAccountability](https://www.bishop-accountability.org/2025/08/another-cyber-breach-threatens-baltimore-catholic-church-abuse-survivors/)). That means diocesan vendor due diligence has to cover law firms and consultants, not only edtech. The second is youth-ministry accounts. Diocesan policies rightly require "two adults with admin access" ([Diocese of San Diego](https://sdcatholic.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/Diocesan-32-Electric-comm-minors-12.3.2025.pdf)), which in practice often becomes one shared password. **Next steps:** write a one-page "Safe Environment coordinator account hygiene" sheet covering MFA on VIRTUS or CMG Connect, offboarding when coordinators change, no emailed SSN spreadsheets, and locked storage and a retention schedule for paper forms. Add a "two adults, two accounts" note for youth-ministry admins. Frame it as completing the Dallas Charter's intent, and offer it to one diocesan Safe Environment office before any public release.

### 4. Turn the insurance renewal into the moment of truth

Insurers already require MFA, EDR and offline backups ([Marsh McLennan Agency](https://www.marshmma.com/us/insights/details/cyber-security-in-schools.html)). Dioceses pass those demands down: the Archdiocese of Atlanta specifies Microsoft Authenticator over SMS ([Atlanta MFA policy](https://communique.archatl.com/wp-content/uploads/Multi-factor-authentication-MFA-policy.pdf)), and the Diocese of Des Moines publishes its full renewal application ([Des Moines application](https://dmdiocese.org/filesimages/Finance/Business%20Office%20Forums/2025%20Cyberliability%20renewal%20application.pdf)). Attention peaks at renewal, so that is when to offer help: "your questionnaire asks about MFA; here is how to make the answer true by Friday." The novel artifact is a **crosswalk** from published diocesan mandates and renewal questions to the project's first-hour click paths, with a short self-check a parish can run before signing. This is the "subsidium" idea from the school-choice notes in a form a risk manager can use. **Next steps:** collect the public diocesan mandates (Seattle, Atlanta, Milwaukee, St. Paul and Minneapolis, Des Moines, Detroit) into a crosswalk table. Then ask one diocesan risk manager a single question: does Catholic Mutual verify any control, or accept attestation? The answer decides whether the long-term pitch is "help parishes comply" or "help the insurer verify." Australia's CEnet, which pairs Catholic education with a sector-wide incident-response partnership ([CEnet](https://cenet.catholic.edu.au/blog/cybercx)), is the model to cite. No US equivalent was found, and NCEA shows no cybersecurity program ([NCEA](https://www.ncea.org/privacy)).

### 5. A payment protocol that a cloned voice cannot pass

Write a one-page policy and a 10-minute drill for the bookkeeper. It should include a 48-hour hold on any bank-detail change, confirmation by callback to the number already on file, notice to the *old* contact, a second approver, and a pre-agreed code word that is never spoken on calls the requester started. Add a Claude role-play exercise in which the "pastor" calls to change payroll details and the bookkeeper practices refusing. Its value is that it targets the 58-60% of losses directly and replaces the "look for bad grammar" training that AI has made obsolete. Public board-meeting recordings and parish livestreams are named as voice-cloning material ([Jamf](https://www.jamf.com/blog/social-engineering-k12-schools-ai/)). **Next steps:** draft it, walk it through with one pilot bookkeeper, and send it to the Catholic press syndicate. OSV News's 2023 parish-cybersecurity piece ran in diocesan papers nationwide ([America](https://www.americamagazine.org/politics-society/2023/05/08/parish-cybersecurity-risk-hope-experts-245247)).

### 6 through 12: the data and oversight bench

Rank 6 extends the planned read-only Workspace posture check to **find likely child-protection files**, meaning documents whose titles or labels suggest counseling, IEP, custody or incident content shared beyond a restricted group. It also adds a breach-day family letter with child-freeze steps. Following the AI research's safe pattern, AI reads and recommends and a human acts, with no standing admin credentials, because AI agents with broad tokens have deleted production data and backups in seconds ([ITPro](https://www.itpro.com/technology/artificial-intelligence/nine-seconds-was-all-it-took-for-an-ai-agent-to-wipe-a-startups-database-experts-warn-its-a-glimpse-into-the-future-challenges-of-identity-security)). The same check can list third-party OAuth app grants, which matters because ISL found unvetted apps outnumber licensed ones about 2 to 1 ([ISL](https://internetsafetylabs.org/blog/research/2022-k-12-edtech-benchmark-revisited-unvetted-off-the-shelf-apps-outnumber-licensed-apps-2-to-1/)).

Rank 7 builds on the NYC work without repeating it. File a FOIL request for the DOE's full incident log: 141 incidents per the Comptroller against 7 public "incidents of note." Publish short pre-vote briefs for the Panel for Educational Policy, which voted down a $1.65M Oracle renewal over data concerns before approving it ([Chalkbeat](https://www.chalkbeat.org/newyork/2026/08/28/nyc-schools-ai-oracle-contract-hr-systems-board-pep-vote/)), cross-checking each vendor on the agenda against the 972-disclosure dataset. Keep a tracker of the DOE's June 2026 promises (a written data classification policy and publication of 6,491 AI comments) ([Council hearing](https://legistar.council.nyc.gov/MeetingDetail.aspx?GUID=A5F0EBEC-3119-44ED-95B0-2027EDFDD22D&ID=1421969&Options=info%7C&Search=)). Two NYC seams deserve mapping. Publicly funded pre-K counts as a "school" under Part 121 ([NYSED Parents' Bill of Rights](https://www.nysed.gov/data-privacy-security/bill-rights-data-privacy-and-security-parents-bill-rights)), so community-based pre-K providers may be held to standards they cannot meet. And the DOE acts as the LEA for nonpublic students in special education ([Bond Schoeneck & King](https://www.bsk.com/uploads/Spotlight-on-New-York-City-Private-Schools.pdf)), which puts Catholic-school children's IEP data in DOE flows that 2-d disclosures never describe.

Rank 8 is an open breach panel to replace the K12 SIX map, which stopped in 2022 ([K12 SIX](https://www.k12six.org/52133ded-1103-4afc-a02e-e83b1e580384)). Maine's portal tags "Entity Type: Education" and requires notice even when one Maine resident is affected, which makes it a national dragnet ([Maine AG](https://www.maine.gov/agviewer/content/ag/985235c7-cb95-4be2-8792-a1252b4f8318/ba471657-be17-411d-a052-4855948d499c.html)). Texas deletes listings after a year ([Texas OAG](https://texasattorneygeneral.gov/consumer-protection/data-breach-reporting)), so weekly archiving preserves a public record that would otherwise vanish. The headline metric would be the vendor-caused share of student records exposed. NYSED reported 662 incident reports in 2025, up 72%, with 230 involving contractors ([DataBreaches.net](https://databreaches.net/2026/04/06/nys-school-data-incidents-rose-72-in-2025-with-44-reported-on-long-island/)).

Rank 9 generalizes the 2-d study to the SDPC registry. Feasibility is scored 3 because signed PDFs sit at patterned URLs, and enumerating unlinked IDs would cross the project's line. The correct path is to contact A4L first and use only public search pages or the documented API ([SDPC API guide](https://files.a4l.org/privacy/SDPC-API/SDPC_Resource_Registry_API_developers_guide.pdf)).

Rank 10 is a five-minute sweep the domain checkup can prompt. Did a nonprofit lose Business Premium? Is protective DNS in place to replace MS-ISAC's domain blocking? Is the school enrolled in CISA Cyber Hygiene ([CISA](https://www.cisa.gov/cyber-hygiene-services))?

Rank 11 waits on states' certified SGO lists, due February 15, 2027, which open a new universe of small nonprofits holding family income data ([IRS FSTC](https://www.irs.gov/charities-non-profits/federal-scholarship-tax-credit-fstc)). An aggregate-only DMARC and privacy-policy baseline, run with the existing checkup, would measure the "$1,700 lure" impersonation risk without naming any SGO.

Rank 12 is a photo standard: no full names with faces, no photos of flagged children, private galleries. It answers both stalkers and deepfake sextortion, since UK schools have pulled pupil photos after criminals scraped them to make explicit fakes ([Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/family-and-parenting/2026/05/deepfake-sextortion-forces-schools-to-remove-student-photos-from-websites)) and NCMEC logged more than 50,000 financial sextortion reports in 2025 ([NCMEC](https://us.missingkids.org/blog/2026/ncmec-releases-new-sextortion-data-2025)).

## Unknowns that could reorder the list

Several of these rankings rest on facts the research could not confirm, and the project should treat them as tests rather than assumptions. The biggest is **whether Catholic Mutual verifies anything**. No underwriting questionnaire was found. If it verifies nothing, direction 4 becomes the highest-impact item, since one insurer's practice reaches thousands of sites. If it already scans, the project should plug into that scanning instead. The second is jurisdiction. Nonprofits are generally believed to sit outside the FTC Act, which would leave nonprofit-run apps and SGOs outside COPPA, but the research did not confirm this against primary text. The extent of 2-d coverage of community-based pre-K needs reading of Ed Law 2-d(1) itself. Both affect how direction 2 is framed. The third is evidence of harm mechanisms. No primary source shows US sextortion offenders choosing victims from leaked school rosters ([NCMEC data](https://www.missingkids.org/cybertiplinedata) and the AP/FRONTLINE work describe volume, not selection). No US school voice-clone wire loss is documented. No US case was found of an abuser locating a child through a leaked school or parish record, as opposed to through intent and ads. The project should present these as precautionary, not proven. Fourth, datasets age. ISL's 96% third-party-sharing figure comes from 2022 data that predates generative-AI features ([ISL benchmark](https://internetsafetylabs.org/resources/reports/2022-us-k12-edtech-benchmark/)), and Common Sense's reasons for pausing are unknown. Finally, CISA Cyber Hygiene's eligibility for private schools and 501(c)(3)s was not confirmed.

The boundaries are not just constraints. They are the project's credibility, and they shape method. Breach panels and DPA studies publish aggregates and name only large vendors and public agencies already subject to public enforcement, as the 2-d study did with Illuminate. Measurement follows the Menlo Report norms: rate limits, an identified crawler, and advance notice to agencies before systemic findings go public ([IRTF PEARG draft](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-irtf-pearg-safe-internet-measurement-14)). Public pages reached without authentication are legally low-risk under hiQ v. LinkedIn ([9th Cir.](https://govinfo.gov/content/pkg/USCOURTS-ca9-17-16783/pdf/USCOURTS-ca9-17-16783-1.pdf)), but privately run registries such as SDPC carry their own terms, which must be read and recorded first. The protected-child kit must never collect or store any child's details, and the Workspace finder must run under the organization's own credentials, read-only. The reach problem also has a ready answer: clinic capacity, not demand, is the bottleneck. Roughly 700 organizations a year are served across 61 clinics ([NYU Center for Cybersecurity](https://cyber.nyu.edu/2026/06/09/generous-gift-from-craig-newmark-philanthropies-funds-first-cyber-clinic-at-nyus-center-for-cybersecurity/)), and the new NYU clinic names schools and nonprofits and coordinates with NYC Cyber Command. Intake-ready versions of directions 1, 3 and 5 make the project a force multiplier for that clinic rather than a competitor.

A 90-day sequence follows from the scores. In December, draft the protected-child checklist and the COPPA vendor letter, and send the one risk-manager question about Catholic Mutual. Separately, the NYC Canvas monitoring enrollment deadline of December 7 ([NYC DOE](https://www.schools.nyc.gov/about-us/policies/data-privacy-and-security-policies/data-security-incidents)) is a timely parent-facing reminder the project could amplify. In January, pilot the payment protocol and the Safe Environment sheet with the three pilot organizations DIRECTION.md already proposes, start the Texas archive, and file the NYC FOIL. By February, have the renewal crosswalk ready for a diocese and the SGO baseline ready for when state lists appear on February 15.

## Conclusion

The project began from "AI shifts security toward defenders" and found that, for organizations with nobody on staff, the defender AI that matters is not a copilot in their tenant, which Microsoft gates to E5 licenses ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/copilot/security/security-copilot-inclusion)). It is a reader that does the checking that attestation systems skipped. That reframes the whole effort. Small child-serving organizations sit in a legal void. They are surrounded by parties that make promises about them: vendors under COPPA, insurers through renewal forms, dioceses through mandates, cities through disclosures. The cheapest high-impact move is to make those promises checkable and to tie each check to a moment when money or permission is at stake. AI makes that reading nearly free, as the 2-d study showed by checking 972 disclosures with a few readable rules.

The second insight is that safeguarding, not cybersecurity, is the language that opens doors and the harm model that matters. A breach that exposes a custody arrangement or an abuse report hurts a child in ways no credit-monitoring offer touches. A court order that does not reach the pickup app is a security failure that no scanner will ever flag. The directions that score highest treat security as the plumbing that makes child-protection commitments real. That is defensible, it is novel, and a single volunteer who already speaks both languages can do it.
