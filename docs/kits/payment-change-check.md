# Payment-change check

*One page. Print it and tape it next to whoever pays the bills and runs payroll.*

## The rule

**No bank detail changes on the strength of a message alone.** Not an email, not a letter, not a phone call, not a text, even from someone you know, even from the boss.

This covers both:

- **vendors** asking you to pay a new account, and
- **staff** asking to change where their paycheck goes.

## Why

- **Dickinson Public Schools, North Dakota (2026):** criminals impersonated a vendor and redirected two scheduled payments, nearly $4.92 million from the building fund ([Valley News Live, Feb 10, 2026](https://www.valleynewslive.com/2026/02/11/north-dakota-school-district-loses-nearly-5-million-sophisticated-email-scam/)). Federal prosecutors later seized $4,856,578.51 traced to the scheme; it stays with the U.S. Marshals until a court rules ([U.S. Attorney, Apr 30, 2026](https://www.justice.gov/usao-nd/pr/north-dakota-fbi-and-us-attorneys-office-recover-48-million-dollars-scammed-dickinson)).
- **St. Ambrose Parish, Brunswick, Ohio (2019):** two staff email accounts were hacked and used to say the building contractor "had changed its bank and wiring instructions." The parish lost $1.75 million ([Catholic News Service via America, Apr 30, 2019](https://www.americamagazine.org/faith/2019/04/30/hackers-infiltrate-ohio-parishs-email-system-steal-175-million); the FBI confirmed it was business email compromise, [FBI Cleveland](https://www.fbi.gov/contact-us/field-offices/cleveland/news/press-releases/st-ambrose-catholic-parish-was-a-victim-of-a-business-email-compromise-scheme)).
- **Sudbury Public Schools, Massachusetts (2025):** attackers changed four employees' direct-deposit details and a payroll specialist approved the changes without verifying them ([Sudbury Weekly, May 7, 2026](https://sudburyweekly.com/sps-hit-by-payroll-cyberattack-in-2025-over-12000-diverted/)).

The FBI counts $55.5 billion in reported business email compromise losses worldwide from 2013 to 2023 and advises: "Use secondary channels and/or two-factor authentication to verify requests for changes in account information." ([FBI IC3, Sept 2024](https://www.ic3.gov/PSA/2024/PSA240911))

## The five steps, every time

1. **Hold for at least 48 hours.** No change takes effect the day it's asked for. Note the date and who asked. Any payment due to that vendor waits too, or goes to the account you already have.
2. **Call back on the number you already had.** Use the phone number in your vendor file or staff file from *before* the request. Never a number, link or contact in the request itself, and never just reply to the email. Caller ID can be faked, so you place the call. (Nacha: confirm changes "outside of the communication method or channel requesting the change" and "call the requester back on a known phone number." [Nacha BEC action plan, 2024](https://www.nacha.org/system/files/2024-07/Business%20Email%20Compromise%20Action%20Plan.pdf))
3. **Ask for the code word.** Each vendor you pay regularly, and each staff member, agrees a code word with you in advance (see the vendor letter below). The person you reach must give it. No code word, no change.
4. **Second person approves.** Someone else on the short list looks at the request, the callback notes and the new details, and signs off. Nacha recommends "dual controls for approval of certain transactions."
5. **Tell the old contact and watch the first payment.** Send a short notice to the vendor's or employee's *previous* email address on file saying the details changed, so a fraud gets noticed fast. Then confirm with the vendor (again, on the known number) that the next payment arrived. For staff, confirm on payday.

Log it: date, who asked, number you called, who answered, code word given (yes/no), who approved.

## Red flags: stop and call your supervisor

- urgency ("must go out today", "account frozen", "audit")
- a new bank in a different state or country
- the boss asking you to keep it quiet or skip the steps
- a voice that sounds right but won't call back on the known number (voices can be cloned)
- a request that comes right before a big scheduled payment

**Nobody gets to skip the steps.** Not the pastor, the principal, or the board chair. Put that in writing so the bookkeeper is never the one who has to argue.

## If money went out wrong

1. **Call your bank's fraud line immediately** and ask them to recall the payment. The FBI: "Immediately contact your financial institution and request a recall of the funds." Speed matters.
2. **File at [ic3.gov](https://www.ic3.gov)** "as soon as possible," whatever the amount.
3. Call your insurer and your diocese, district or board.
4. Don't reset passwords or delete emails until someone has saved the evidence (sign-in logs, the emails).

## Vendor letter (send once to every vendor you pay regularly)

> Subject: How we'll confirm any change to your payment details
>
> Hello,
>
> To protect both of us from payment fraud, [Organization] has a simple rule: we never change a vendor's bank details based on an email, letter or call alone.
>
> If your bank details ever change, we will call you back at the number we already have on file, [number], and ask for a code word that only our two offices know. Please reply by phone (not email) to agree a code word with [Name] at [our main number]. A second person here will then approve the change, and we'll confirm with you after the first payment.
>
> If you ever get an email that looks like it's from us asking you to change how *we* pay *you*, or asking for anything unusual, please call us at [our main number] before acting.
>
> Thank you for helping us keep everyone's money safe.
>
> [Name]
> [Title], [Organization]

Do the same with staff at onboarding: their code word goes in the confidential HR file, never in email.

## Talking to your bank (one meeting)

Ask your bank these questions. Not every bank offers every service, and some charge for them.

1. **Two-person approval:** can our online banking require a second person to approve any new payee, any change to a payee's account, and any wire or ACH over a set amount?
2. **Alerts:** can we get text or email alerts for new payees, changed payees and outgoing wires? (Nacha recommends activity alerts and reviewing statements daily.)
3. **Positive Pay and ACH debit blocks:** do you offer check Positive Pay, ACH Positive Pay, or ACH debit blocks or filters?
4. **Recall:** if we send money to a fraudster, what is your direct fraud line, what hours, and how fast can you try a recall?
5. **Payroll provider:** does our payroll service let us require a second approval for direct-deposit changes, and notify the employee at their old account and address?
