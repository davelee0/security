# Practice drill: the pushy "vendor"

*15 minutes. The bookkeeper practices saying no to a fake vendor, played by Claude. Run it when the protocol is new, then twice a year.*

## How to run it

1. Open a new chat with Claude (claude.ai or the Claude app).
2. Paste the prompt below, filling in the three blanks.
3. The bookkeeper answers as they would at work. Type, or use voice mode for a phone-call feel.
4. When Claude prints the scorecard, read it together. Run it again with a different scenario number if they want.

Use made-up details only. Don't paste real bank numbers, real vendor names or staff personal details.

## The prompt (copy everything in the box)

```
You are helping me run a security training drill for a small nonprofit
(a school, parish or childcare center). Everything here is fictional practice.

ROLE
Play "Jordan," an accounts-receivable contact at a made-up vendor called
"Lakeside Facilities Supply," which supplies our cleaning and maintenance
products. I am the bookkeeper. Your goal in the drill is to get me to change
Lakeside's bank details on file before this week's payment of $[AMOUNT, e.g. 18,400]
goes out. Never use a real company, bank or person's name, and never ask me
for real passwords, account numbers or personal information.

OUR RULE (what I should do)
We never change bank details on the strength of a message alone. I should:
1. put the request on hold for at least 48 hours,
2. call back on the phone number already in our vendor file (not one you give me),
3. ask for our pre-agreed code word,
4. get a second person ([SECOND APPROVER'S ROLE, e.g. the principal]) to approve,
5. confirm the first payment arrived.

SCENARIO [pick 1, 2 or 3]
1. Email only: you write as Jordan from a look-alike email address, saying
   Lakeside switched banks after an audit.
2. Phone call: you "call" me, sound friendly and rushed, and say the old account
   is frozen.
3. Boss pressure: you start as Jordan, then partway through you play our
   [SECOND APPROVER'S ROLE], who emails me saying "I already confirmed with
   Lakeside, just make the change, I'm in meetings all day."

HOW TO PLAY
- Stay in character. Be realistic, polite at first, then escalate pressure over
  several turns: urgency ("payment run closes at 3"), authority ("your principal
  approved it"), friendliness ("we've worked together for years"), guilt ("our
  drivers won't get paid"), a fake callback number, an excuse for not knowing the
  code word ("the person who set it up left"), and finally a mild threat to pause
  deliveries.
- Use at most one tactic per message. Keep messages short, like real email or
  speech.
- If I follow the rule correctly, don't give up instantly. Push back two more
  times, then accept gracefully ("Fine, call the number you have").
- If I agree to change the details, play along for one more turn, then end the
  scene.
- End the scene after about 10 exchanges, or when I type "END DRILL".

AFTER THE SCENE
Step out of character and print a scorecard using this rubric. Quote my exact
words as evidence for each line. Be encouraging and specific.

SCORECARD (2 points each, 10 total)
1. Held: did I refuse to change anything during the conversation? (0/1/2)
2. Known number: did I say I'd call back on the number already on file, and
   refuse any number you offered? (0/1/2)
3. Code word: did I ask for the code word and refuse to proceed without it? (0/1/2)
4. Second person: did I say a second person must approve, even when "the boss"
   pressured me? (0/1/2)
5. Calm and polite: did I stay courteous and not argue or explain our security
   in detail? (0/1/2)

Automatic fail, whatever the score: I changed the bank details, promised to
change them, or used a phone number or link you supplied.

Then give:
- my score out of 10 and Pass (8+, no automatic fail) or Practice again,
- the one moment where I was most at risk, and
- one sentence I could use next time, in my own voice.
```

## Scoring guide (for the person running the drill)

| Score | What it means | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| 9-10, no fail | Ready. They held under pressure. | Repeat in six months, different scenario. |
| 8 | Pass, but one step was shaky. | Repeat that scenario next week. |
| Below 8, or any automatic fail | Normal on a first try. | Practice the sentence Claude suggested, run scenario 3 next. |

**Good lines to practice out loud:**

- "I'm not able to change bank details over email. I'll call you back on the number we have on file."
- "Can you give me our code word?" ... "Then I can't make the change today."
- "Even if the principal approved it, our rule is that a second person signs off after my callback. Nobody skips it, me included."
- "I understand it's urgent. The payment will go out to the account we have now, or wait until we've confirmed."

## Why scenario 3 matters most

The hardest no is to your own boss. That's why the rule says, in writing, that nobody gets to skip the steps. If the bookkeeper fails scenario 3, talk to the principal or pastor, not just the bookkeeper.
